## Highlights

### 1. JASN publishes the largest externally-validated kidney failure prediction model for early ADPKD

- **What happened:** Ravichandran et al. (JASN, May 2026, pm_41563252) developed a multivariable Cox model for kidney failure (eGFR <15, dialysis, or transplant) using age, sex, eGFR, ADPKD genotype, and Mayo Imaging Class in the CRISP+HALT-A development cohort (n=759, baseline eGFR 91 mL/min/1.73 m², median 10.2-year follow-up), and externally validated it in the Mayo ADPKD clinical registry (ages 15–49, eGFR ≥60).
- **Why people may care:** Early-stage ADPKD risk stratification is a long-standing unmet need driving tolvaptan eligibility, trial enrichment, and patient counselling. This is the largest externally-validated effort to date that combines routinely-available clinical data with Mayo Imaging Class for the patient-important endpoint of kidney failure.
- **What limits confidence:** Observational design with no intervention arm; external validation cohort restricted to ages 15–49 with preserved eGFR (≥60), so generalizability to older patients, advanced disease, and non-Caucasian populations remains to be demonstrated; downstream calibration drift in non-research settings has not been tested. Practice change should await independent reproduction in non-CRISP cohorts and EHR-integrated operationalization.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant
- **Scores:** Importance 8/10 | Evidence 6/10 | Novelty 8/10 | Decision 7/10 | Calibration 8/10

### 2. CJASN provides the first quantitative TKV-to-eGFR translation framework for ADPKD accelerated approval

- **What happened:** Yu et al. (CJASN, Feb 13 2026, pm_41686506; surfaced in our scan window) used intraindividual mixed-effects modeling on CRISP and HALT-PKD Study A to estimate that within Mayo Imaging Classes 1C–1E, each 1 percentage-point/year reduction in TKV growth rate corresponds to ~0.40–0.52 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr slower eGFR decline. The authors explicitly position the model as a framework for FDA accelerated-approval discussions in ADPKD.
- **Why people may care:** This directly addresses the regulatory science gap that has constrained ADPKD drug development since TKV was accepted as a "reasonably likely" surrogate. Quantitative targets reshape go/no-go decisions and trial design for the active Phase 2/3 pipeline (VX-407, ABBV-CLS-628, PYC-003, JMKX003142, farabursen Phase 2, CAM2029, tamibarotene).
- **What limits confidence:** Built on observational CRISP and HALT-PKD A intraindividual slopes, not on randomized treatment effects; generalizability to Mayo class 2 (atypical/non-PKD1), very advanced 1E patients, and non-cAMP mechanisms is untested. The translation ratio is an estimate with uncertainty bounds. Crucially, this does NOT yet constitute FDA acceptance of any specific surrogate threshold — it is a framework, not a regulatory decision.
- **Classification:** pipeline-relevant
- **Scores:** Importance 8/10 | Evidence 6/10 | Novelty 7/10 | Decision 8/10 | Calibration 9/10

### 3. Real-world UK tolvaptan effectiveness is smaller than RCT estimates and largely capped by early discontinuation

- **What happened:** Marshall et al. (Am J Nephrology, Feb 2026, pm_41758739) report a UK single-centre propensity score-weighted retrospective cohort: 327 ADPKD patients (119 tolvaptan-treated vs 208 untreated), mean follow-up 61.4 months. Primary chronic eGFR slope difference was +0.45 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr (95% CI −0.37 to +1.18) — not statistically significant; the CI is compatible with no effect. 52.1% of tolvaptan patients discontinued within 12 months, predominantly because of aquaretic side effects.
- **Why people may care:** TEMPO 3:4's eGFR slope effect (~1 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr) is the headline number patients hear. Real-world UK data suggest the average treated effect is meaningfully smaller, with treatment discontinuation as a likely major driver. This is decision-relevant for shared decision-making and for adherence-supportive strategies (e.g., HCTZ adjuvant in HYDROPROTECT NCT05373264).
- **What limits confidence:** Single-centre, retrospective, propensity-weighted (does not eliminate confounding by indication); informative censoring; survivor bias in long-term subgroup estimates (where the per-protocol-like estimate climbs to +0.58 to +0.78 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr); CI on primary outcome includes null. The companion C-MAJOR Canadian registry (n=470, McFarlane et al., Can J Kidney Health Dis Jan 2026, pm_41541406) is a baseline-characteristics paper without long-term outcomes yet.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 5/10 | Decision 6/10 | Calibration 9/10

## Negative / Null Results This Week

**Marshall et al. real-world tolvaptan (pm_41758739):** the primary chronic eGFR slope effect did not reach statistical significance (95% CI −0.37 to +1.18 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr). The CI is compatible with no effect, although the point estimate and subgroup analyses are directionally consistent with TEMPO 3:4. The decision-relevant signal here is NOT "tolvaptan does not work" — that would over-interpret a single propensity-weighted single-centre cohort. The fair read is: average real-world effectiveness is smaller than RCT estimates, and >50% one-year discontinuation is the dominant operational driver.

**Tantush et al. SGLT2i meta-analysis non-diabetic subgroup (ss_136bc72):** the non-diabetic ADPKD subgroup showed numerically larger but statistically non-significant eGFR slope benefit. This is a soft negative within a positive overall meta-analysis and is consistent with the limited size of dedicated non-diabetic ADPKD evidence. STOP-PKD remains the decisive test.

No high-decision-value class-pruning negative results this cycle (in contrast to W16, where the octreotide LAR meta-analysis materially pruned somatostatin analogues from the ADPKD disease-modifying space).

## Evolving Understanding

### Risk prediction in early ADPKD takes a meaningful step forward
The Ravichandran JASN model is the largest externally-validated prognostic effort yet for the patient-important kidney-failure endpoint in early ADPKD. Combined with the TKV+KFRE validation paper (ss_0f028a9d) for advanced disease and the Yu CJASN translation framework (pm_41686506) for trial-design, this cycle adds three orthogonal pieces to the ADPKD risk-prediction toolkit:
1. **Yu (CJASN)** — population-level: how much TKV slowing should a drug deliver to plausibly translate into eGFR benefit (regulatory)?
2. **Ravichandran (JASN)** — individual-level: what is this early-disease patient's risk of kidney failure given age/sex/eGFR/genotype/Mayo class?
3. **TKV+KFRE (KI Reports)** — individual-level: in advanced disease (eGFR<60), KFRE adds discrimination beyond Mayo Imaging Class alone.

Practice change should still wait for independent reproduction in non-CRISP cohorts and EHR integration. But the field's risk-prediction infrastructure is sharper this cycle than it was last cycle.

### Real-world tolvaptan — narrowing the trial-to-practice gap
Marshall et al. (UK propensity-weighted) and McFarlane et al. (C-MAJOR Canadian registry baseline) build out the picture started by the prior UK prescribing-variation paper (ss_1f4af4a, W16). Two takeaways:
- Real-world average effect on eGFR slope is smaller than TEMPO 3:4, with the CI containing zero in the UK single-centre data.
- Discontinuation (52% within 12 months) is the dominant driver of attenuated effectiveness — making adherence-supportive strategies (HCTZ adjuvant in HYDROPROTECT, structured aquaretic-burden monitoring tools like the PKD Daily diary) more relevant.

This does not undermine the trial evidence base. It refines clinical-counseling expectations and supports prioritizing tolerability research.

### SGLT2 inhibitors — first ADPKD-specific meta-analysis is consistent with class effect, not ADPKD-specific signal
Tantush et al.'s pooled +1.34 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr eGFR-slope attenuation lines up with the SGLT2i CKD class-effect (~1 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr). The non-diabetic ADPKD subgroup did not reach significance. This is consistent with prior moderate-confidence enthusiasm but does not raise it. STOP-PKD (Phase 3, n=420) remains the trial that determines whether SGLT2i should become standard of care for non-diabetic ADPKD on top of tolvaptan or as an alternative.

### Polycystin restoration — preclinical pieces accumulating around the farabursen thesis
The Lakhia bioRxiv preprint (six-nucleotide miRNA motif disruption raises PKD1 dosage in mice), the JASN INPP5E/PIPKIγ ciliary phosphoinositide work, and the Fedeles chemical-chaperones angle from W16 collectively reinforce that polycystin-restoration is mechanistically tractable from multiple directions. None of these is clinically actionable. They sharpen the research case for the Vertex VX-407 corrector (PKD1 missense), Regulus farabursen (miR-17), and PYC-003 (RNA modulator) programs.

### Phosphate, anemia, osmolality — three modifiable-biology hypotheses, none yet practice-changing
- **Phosphate (pm_42024440):** preclinical evidence that high dietary phosphate accelerates cyst growth in Pkd1RC/RC mice; calcium-phosphate microcrystals seen in human PKD imaging. Hypothesis-generating; ADPKD translation history weak.
- **Anemia (pm_42053857):** Japanese single-centre observational; lower Hb associated with faster decline, with sex-specific risk weighting. Insufficient to change anemia treatment thresholds.
- **Osmolality (pm_41678280):** Hong Kong CysticHK cohort; high serum osmolality predicts faster decline. Does NOT establish that water intake or vasopressin antagonism for osmolality lowering improves outcomes — PREVENT-ADPKD water-intake RCT was historically negative on TKV.

These three findings sketch a "modifiable physiology" research agenda but do not translate into clinic actions yet.

### KDIGO 2025 implementation
ERA Working Group's commentary on the ICA/vascular section (pm_42065712) is the most clinically useful regional adaptation since the KDOQI commentary (W16). Practical clinical implications: screen ICA selectively (family history, smoking, BP), prefer contrast-free MRA, screen relatives for thoracic aortic aneurysm but not abdominal. CARI's commentary (pm_42011799) translates to AU/NZ.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **pm_42050863 (Hanaoka tolvaptan diurnal BP, AJH April 28 2026).** Single-arm pre/post n=23 reports an annual TKV growth rate falling from 13.6%/yr to 1.61%/yr and eGFR slope improving from −4.46 to −2.26 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr. These magnitudes are biologically implausible for a pre/post observational study of this size — they would dwarf the TEMPO 3:4 effect. Likely explanations: regression to the mean (patients started tolvaptan after a rapid-decline window), selection (patients tolerating tolvaptan for follow-up are the responders), and possibly measurement noise in TKV. The BP-dipper finding may be physiologically real (vasopressin plays a role in nocturnal BP) but the kidney endpoints from this design cannot anchor any clinical claim. Claim calibration: 5/10. Treat as background only; do not let any review or media coverage frame this as "tolvaptan slows ADPKD progression by 8-fold."

2. **pm_41843812 (Reversible cystogenesis primate model).** "Reversible cystogenesis" is a strong framing for what is, in practice, smaller-cyst regression observed on serial ultrasound in a small primate cohort. Without histological confirmation, regression-to-the-mean and measurement error are plausible alternatives. The model is genuinely useful — non-rodent ADPKD platforms are valuable — but the headline outpaces the data.

3. **ss_136bc72 (Tantush Cureus SGLT2i SR/MA).** Cureus is a low-prestige, broad-acceptance open-access journal. The pooled effect aligns with the SGLT2i CKD class effect, not an ADPKD-specific signal. Use it as a synthesis pointer, not as the basis for clinical recommendations on top of standard CKD care.

4. **ss_cfaaa122 (WCN26-4375 C-MAJOR PROs abstract).** Tolvaptan-sponsored conference abstract reporting selected PRO endpoints from a commercial registry. Wait for full peer-reviewed long-term outcome reports.

5. **pm_42050863 framing language (and any future framing of n=20-30 single-arm pre/post tolvaptan retrospective papers).** A pattern is emerging of small Japanese single-centre tolvaptan series claiming dramatic slope improvements (cf. Jankowska n=20 in Medicina, W16). All such studies should default to n=20-30 retrospective pre/post = hypothesis-generating evidence value 2/10.

6. **JASN preclinical cluster (integrin α1β1, α-Klotho, INPP5E/PIPKIγ, ALG8 reprogramming).** A high-profile journal in one issue does not change ADPKD's preclinical-to-clinical translation track record. These are research-priority signals, not clinical confidence builders.
