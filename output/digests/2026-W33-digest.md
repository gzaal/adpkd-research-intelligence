## Highlights

*Only two items met the highlight bar this cycle. Nothing here should change practice today; the first item should change how confidently a clinician talks about SGLT2 inhibitors in ADPKD.*

### 1. Second ADPKD-specific SGLT2i meta-analysis: the eGFR benefit is significant only against patients' own pre-treatment slope, and disappears against a real comparator group — TKV null

- **What happened:** A PRISMA-compliant, PROSPERO-registered meta-analysis (Kanbay et al., *Diabetes Research and Clinical Practice*, epub 2026-08-12; CRD420261295918; 6 studies, 451 ADPKD patients) found first-year eGFR-slope attenuation with SGLT2 inhibitors that was statistically significant **only** versus each patient's own pre-SGLT2i slope (mean difference +0.65 mL/min/1.73 m²/yr, 95% CI 0.05–1.26), and **not significant** versus a non-SGLT2i-treated ADPKD comparator group (+2.12, 95% CI −8.13 to 12.38). Total kidney volume change was also null (−31.31 mL/yr, 95% CI −184.00 to 121.37). The authors conclude that "current evidence is insufficient to establish a beneficial effect of SGLT2 inhibitors on kidney outcomes in patients with ADPKD," and report no major safety concerns.
- **Why people may care:** SGLT2 inhibitors are already licensed, cheap, familiar, and increasingly prescribed off-label in ADPKD on the strength of the general CKD class effect. This is the first ADPKD-specific synthesis to place the weak (pre/post) and strong (between-group) comparisons side by side — and the result is that the encouraging number comes from the weak design and does not survive the strong one.
- **What limits confidence:** Six mostly observational studies, a small pooled sample (n=451), surrogate endpoints only, and one year of follow-up in a disease that progresses over decades. The between-group confidence interval is very wide (−8.13 to 12.38), so this is an *absence of demonstrated benefit in sparse controlled data*, not a demonstration of absence of benefit. It also does not address the dialysis-initiation endpoint. Declared conflicts: one author reports consultancy/speaker support from both SGLT2i and tolvaptan manufacturers — noted, though the null direction of the result makes sponsorship-driven inflation implausible here.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant (informs counseling and prescribing restraint; does not settle efficacy)
- **Scores:** Importance 7/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 7/10 | Decision 7/10 | Calibration 9/10

> **Correction to the scan-pass assessment.** The 2026-08-15 scan scored this paper's headline-vs-evidence as "overstated" and calibration 6/10. Full-abstract review shows the opposite: the paper's own conclusion is appropriately conservative and matches its data. The paper record has been corrected (calibration 6→9, evidence 4→5, decision 5→7). The overclaiming risk in this literature lies with the secondary citation of the pooled pre/post figure, not with this paper.

### 2. Ferritin/iron handling is disrupted in polycystic kidneys — but deleting ferritin heavy chain did not change cyst growth

- **What happened:** In PKD mice (Sommer et al., *Am J Physiol Renal Physiol*, epub 2026-08-09), ferritin was upregulated in cyst-lining epithelium and macrophages in both mouse and human kidneys, and infused ferritin accumulated abnormally in PKD but not wild-type kidneys — indicating genuinely dysfunctional iron trafficking. The study's direct functional tests were negative: conditional ferritin heavy chain (FTH) knockout in collecting duct **or** myeloid cells did not alter renal cyst growth, and neither ferritin nor apoferritin infusion changed cyst progression.
- **Why people may care:** Iron and ferritin dysregulation is a fashionable candidate mechanism across kidney disease. A clean negative functional test in the relevant model deprioritises FTH as a direct druggable cyst-growth target before it attracts a translational program.
- **What limits confidence:** Mouse-model only, in a disease with a poor preclinical-to-clinical translation record. Two qualifications stop this from being a clean refutation: FTH deletion produced compensatory ferritin *light* chain upregulation that could mask a true effect, and only two cell lineages were targeted. Note also a headline-vs-evidence mismatch — the abstract's closing sentence asserts that altered iron trafficking "promotes localized oxidative stress and fibrosis contributing to disease progression," which is associative and sits in tension with the paper's own null functional experiments.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating (with negative-result value for research prioritisation, not for patient care)
- **Scores:** Importance 4/10 | Evidence 4/10 | Novelty 6/10 | Decision 3/10 | Calibration 6/10

*No third item cleared the highlight bar. The polycystin-1/endothelium paper below is mechanistically interesting but entirely preclinical, and this cycle produced no pipeline milestone.*

## New Papers — By Dimension

### Pharmacological

**pm_42586506 — SGLT2i systematic review and meta-analysis, 6 studies / n=451 (Kanbay et al.)** [practice-relevant] — See Highlight 1. Drove the finding_005 downgrade. Scores: Imp 7 | Ev 5 | Nov 7 | Dec 7 | Cal 9.

**pm_42584781 — SGLT2i in ADPKD: rationale, evidence gaps and ongoing trials (Spatola et al., *Int Urol Nephrol*, epub 2026-08-12)** [background/context]
Narrative review, no new data. Its substantive point is well made and worth carrying: ADPKD patients were *excluded* from the pivotal SGLT2i CKD RCTs — specifically over concerns about eGFR decline, cyst growth and TKV increase — so the class-effect extrapolation clinicians rely on has never been tested in this population. Correctly frames observational data as showing both beneficial and adverse signals. **Limits:** non-systematic review; superseded on the quantitative question by the companion meta-analysis above. Scores: Imp 4 | Ev 3 | Nov 4 | Dec 3 | Cal 8.

**pm_42579854 — Kidney volume *reduction* on MRI during tolvaptan, 4-patient case series (Koçyiğit et al., *J Nephrol*, epub 2026-08-11)** [hypothesis-generating]
Four patients showed an absolute decrease in height-adjusted TKV on serial MRI (2020–2025), rather than the slowed *increase* seen in TEMPO 3:4/REPRISE; the largest reduction occurred in the patient with the highest baseline htTKV. eGFR change was minimal and did not track the volume change (−9 to 0 mL/min/1.73 m²). **Limits:** n=4, uncontrolled, retrospective, baseline-only, with no denominator for how often this occurs — an anecdote about response heterogeneity, not evidence that tolvaptan shrinks kidneys. Deliberately *not* added to finding_001's supporting papers; logged as an evolution note only. Scores: Imp 2 | Ev 1 | Nov 6 | Dec 1 | Cal 9.

### Genetics / Mechanism

**pm_42573181 — Polycystin-1 protects endothelium from apoptosis and atherosclerosis (Tardajos Ayllon et al., *Cardiovascular Research*, epub 2026-08-10)** [hypothesis-generating]
A zebrafish endothelial mechanoreceptor screen identified PKD1/PKD2 as anti-apoptotic; inducible endothelial-cell-specific *Pkd1* loss — but **not** *Pkd2* loss — increased atherosclerosis in mice, and PKD1 knockdown in human aortic endothelial cells increased apoptosis and reduced eNOS via THBS1/CCN1. New **finding_031** (LOW confidence). The PKD1-vs-PKD2 dissociation is the interesting part and is testable in genotype-stratified human cardiovascular cohorts. **Limits:** entirely zebrafish/mouse/cell-line; no human ADPKD outcome data; changes nothing about cardiovascular screening or management in ADPKD today. Scores: Imp 5 | Ev 3 | Nov 7 | Dec 2 | Cal 7.

**pm_42572368 — Ferritin and iron homeostasis in PKD (Sommer et al.)** [hypothesis-generating, negative functional result] — See Highlight 2. New **finding_032** (LOW confidence). Scores: Imp 4 | Ev 4 | Nov 6 | Dec 3 | Cal 6.

**pm_42597841 — Kidney-specific *Bicc1* deletion produces an ADPKD-like cystogenic program (Gagnieux et al., *iScience*, epub 2026-08-03)** [hypothesis-generating]
Adult inducible tubule-wide *Bicc1* deletion produced few cysts over 6 months, whereas distal-nephron-specific *in utero* deletion produced aggressive PKD-like disease — a developmental-timing dependence that is itself informative. Transcriptional profiling showed overlap with ADPKD and injury-repair signatures, including cAMP and YAP activation. **Limits:** mouse model of a gene distinct from PKD1/PKD2; a research tool for studying convergent cystogenic pathways, with no bearing on patient care and no therapeutic agent tested. No new finding created — logged against the existing mechanistic literature. Scores: Imp 4 | Ev 3 | Nov 6 | Dec 2 | Cal 8.

**pm_42586231 — PKHD1 enhancer collapse and chromosomal instability in clear cell renal cell carcinoma (Wang et al., *Exp Cell Res*, epub 2026-08-12)** [background/context — logged per ARPKD-adjacency rule, not ADPKD news]
Concerns *PKHD1* (the autosomal **recessive** PKD gene) as a centrosomal tumour suppressor in sporadic kidney cancer. Genetically and clinically distinct from ADPKD's PKD1/PKD2 cystogenesis. Retained for completeness only. Scores: Imp 1 | Ev 2 | Nov 3 | Dec 1 | Cal 8.

## Negative / Null Results This Week

**This was, unusually, a week defined by its null results — and they are the most useful thing in it.**

1. **SGLT2 inhibitors, controlled comparison and TKV: both null** (pm_42586506). This is not an RCT and should not be described as one, but it is the most methodologically informative ADPKD-specific SGLT2i evidence published so far, precisely because it separates the pre/post comparison from the between-group comparison. **What it answers:** whether the existing observational ADPKD SGLT2i literature, pooled, demonstrates kidney protection. It does not. **What it rules out:** it rules out treating the pooled eGFR-slope figure as evidence of ADPKD-specific benefit; the significance is an artefact of the weaker design. **What population:** ADPKD patients across the 6 included studies, mostly observational, one year of follow-up. **What remains uncertain:** almost everything that matters — long-term effect, effect on dialysis initiation (not addressed here), effect in nondiabetic subgroups, and effect as add-on to tolvaptan. The between-group interval is wide enough (−8.13 to 12.38) to contain a clinically meaningful benefit as well as a clinically meaningful harm. This weakens the case for off-label use; it does not establish that SGLT2 inhibitors are ineffective in ADPKD, and it says nothing about their established benefit in CKD generally.

2. **Ferritin heavy chain deletion did not alter cyst growth** (pm_42572368). A direct, well-targeted functional test that came out null in two separate cell lineages, plus a null infusion experiment. **What it rules out:** FTH in collecting duct and myeloid compartments as a primary driver of cyst growth in this mouse model. **What remains uncertain:** compensatory ferritin light chain upregulation may have masked an effect, and broader iron-driven oxidative stress and fibrosis remain plausible secondary contributors.

3. **A partial null worth noting** (pm_42597841): adult tubule-wide *Bicc1* deletion produced *few* cysts over 6 months. The aggressive phenotype required in-utero, distal-nephron-restricted deletion. Reported plainly by the authors; a useful reminder that developmental timing and nephron segment drive cystic phenotypes in mouse models in ways that complicate translation to adult-onset human ADPKD.

## Evolving Understanding

**1. SGLT2 inhibitors (finding_005) — CONFIDENCE DOWNGRADED, moderate → LOW.** This is the substantive change this cycle, and it is a reassessment of accumulated evidence rather than a reaction to one paper. Four threads now converge:

- The pre/post design that generated the encouraging pooled eGFR signal was flagged as a weakness back on 2026-05-03. Kanbay now shows directly that the signal does not survive a genuine comparator.
- The Naeem review (W32, 2026-08-09) already found structural/TKV effects "slight or inconsistent." Kanbay quantifies that as null.
- The TriNetX HR 0.35 dialysis result — the single patient-important-endpoint finding that most justified the earlier "moderate" rating — remains under an **unresolved corrigendum** (see Skepticism Notes #1).
- Two meta-analyses of substantially the same literature reached materially different conclusions from very different pooled samples (Tantush n=3,180 vs Kanbay n=451). That divergence is itself evidence that the underlying literature is too heterogeneous and selection-dependent to sustain a moderate-confidence claim.

**What LOW confidence means here, stated plainly for the patient reader:** it means "plausible, mechanistically well-motivated, worth testing, not yet shown to work in ADPKD." It does **not** mean SGLT2 inhibitors have been shown to be ineffective, and it does not touch their well-established benefit in chronic kidney disease generally — a person with ADPKD who is taking one for another accepted indication has no new reason for concern from this week's data. What is no longer supportable is describing off-label SGLT2i as established ADPKD-specific kidney protection. **STOP-PKD (dapagliflozin, n=420) remains the decisive test.**

**2. Tolvaptan (finding_001) — confidence UNCHANGED (high).** A 4-patient case series of absolute kidney-volume reduction was logged as an evolution note only and deliberately kept out of the supporting-paper set. It illustrates response heterogeneity and the imperfect coupling between volumetric and functional response; it carries no weight against the RCT evidence base.

**3. Two new LOW-confidence preclinical findings opened.** finding_031 (polycystin-1 protects vascular endothelium; PKD1 but not PKD2) offers a candidate explanation for ADPKD's extrarenal cardiovascular burden and a testable genotype-stratified prediction in humans. finding_032 (ferritin/iron) is recorded specifically because its informative content is negative. Neither should raise clinical confidence about anything: ADPKD's preclinical-to-clinical translation record is weak, and both are animal/cell-line studies.

**4. No prior finding other than finding_005 changed confidence this cycle.**

## Skepticism Notes

1. **The TriNetX corrigendum remains unresolved after a second cycle of trying.** pm_42473614 corrects pm_41853136 (Chang et al., TriNetX real-world SGLT2i study — the source of the HR 0.35 dialysis-initiation figure cited throughout the knowledge base). It was checked again this cycle via PubMed efetch and PMC (PMC13380713): the corrigendum is deposited **PDF-only with no machine-readable body text**, so its content still cannot be verified programmatically. It is therefore still unknown whether the correction is trivial (affiliation, funding statement) or affects the effect estimate. **Do not cite HR 0.35 without a human reading the corrigendum PDF.** This matters more now than it did two weeks ago, because with the pooled eGFR signal reduced to a pre/post artefact, that HR was carrying disproportionate weight in the SGLT2i case.

2. **A calibration correction in our own favour, not the paper's.** The scan pass scored pm_42586506's headline-vs-evidence as "overstated." Full-abstract review shows the opposite — the authors explicitly conclude that evidence is insufficient. The record has been corrected (calibration 6→9). Flagged here because the failure mode is worth naming: an automated pass can mistake *a paper reporting a weak result honestly* for *a paper making a weak claim*. The overclaiming risk in this literature is in downstream citation of the pooled pre/post figure, not in this paper.

3. **Two of seven papers carried incorrect DOIs from the scan pass — a data-integrity issue, now fixed.** pm_42584781 was stored with DOI `10.1016/j.kint.2024.07.010` (an unrelated *Kidney International* 2024 article) and pm_42597841 with `10.1093/nar/gkad347` (an unrelated *Nucleic Acids Research* article); pm_42597841 also carried a publication date of 2026-08-21, five days in the future. All three were verified against PubMed esummary and corrected in `papers.json`. These are plausible-looking but wrong identifiers — the failure mode where a generated DOI is syntactically valid and points at a real but different paper. **Recommend adding a DOI round-trip validation step to the scan pass** (resolve each stored DOI back to its title before writing).

4. **Two scheduled scans did not run this week.** `output/logs/scan-20260811.log` and `scan-20260813.log` contain no scan output — both record the agent replying that it received only system context with no task prompt. This is a *different* failure mode from the known OAuth-expiry 401 stub (74/100-byte logs): the agent started successfully but the scan prompt was not delivered. The 2026-08-15 run then covered the full 08-08 → 08-15 window, so no papers appear to have been lost, but the alerting latency on any high-importance paper published 08-09 to 08-14 was up to six days instead of two. `output/logs/deep-20260816.log` is also 0 bytes for today's run. **Recommend checking the launchd plist argument passing for `com.adpkd.scan`.**

   **Related, found while running this cycle's sync step:** `scripts/sync.py` now resolves its Google Drive destination from the `ADPKD_GDRIVE_PATH` environment variable, falling back to a `GoogleDrive-YOUR_EMAIL` placeholder path (introduced by commit b7b1800, which removed hardcoded emails from the repo). That variable is **not set in `com.adpkd.scan.plist`, `com.adpkd.deep.plist`, `scan.sh`, or `deep.sh`**, so any automated sync fails with a "folder not found" error and exits 0 — a silent failure. This cycle's sync was completed manually with the variable supplied. **Recommend setting `ADPKD_GDRIVE_PATH` in the launchd plists (or in `scan.sh`/`deep.sh`) before the next scheduled run.**

5. **A note on the ferritin paper's closing claim.** See Highlight 2. The study's data support "iron handling is disrupted in PKD kidneys"; they do not support "this contributes to disease progression," since every direct functional test of that proposition was null. Rescored to calibration 6/10.

6. **Nothing this cycle was stale or recycled.** All 7 papers are genuine first publications within or immediately before the digest window — a welcome contrast to W31 and W32, where re-indexed preprint duplicates were the dominant scan artefact. The deduplication fix recommended at W32 appears to have held, though a 7-paper week is a weak test of it.
