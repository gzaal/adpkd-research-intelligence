## Highlights

### 1. ADPKD carries ~76% elevated aortic dissection/aneurysm risk — large-scale Japanese epidemiological data [STALE — ePublish March 2026, now in print June 2026]

> **Staleness note:** This paper (Miyamoto et al., AJKD) was published online in March 2026 and surfaces now via print-issue batch indexing. It is NOT a new scientific event from this week. It is incorporated here because it has not previously appeared in any digest cycle and adds quantitative population-level support to the cardiovascular surveillance guidance already in our knowledge base.

- **What happened:** A Japanese nationwide claims-database analysis (n=2,570,000+; pm_41881380) compared aortic dissection and aneurysm incidence in ADPKD patients against the general population. Adjusted hazard ratio for aortic events in ADPKD: approximately 1.76.
- **Why people may care:** The ERA Working Group KDIGO 2025 commentary (already incorporated in finding_007) states that "thoracic aortic aneurysm is clearly associated with ADPKD." This nationwide cohort quantifies that association at population scale: a 76% relative elevation above baseline. The data support thoracic aortic surveillance conversations with patients who have family history of aortic complications, or who are undergoing shared decision-making about cardiovascular risk management.
- **What limits confidence:** Retrospective claims-database study; disease coding accuracy for ADPKD in Japanese administrative data may be imperfect; blood pressure control, medication use, and other confounders are incompletely captured in claims data; the cohort is Japanese, and whether HR 1.76 applies equally to European or North American ADPKD populations is uncertain. The ERA KDIGO distinction (thoracic elevated; abdominal NOT elevated) is not specifically addressed in this abstract summary. This paper is stale — it adds quantitative support but does not change the direction of guidance already established.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant (supportive epidemiology for cardiovascular surveillance counseling)
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 4/10 | Novelty 4/10 (STALE) | Decision 5/10 | Calibration 8/10

## New Papers — By Dimension

### Pharmacological / Novel Mechanisms

**pm_42117913 — Zhang et al., FASEB Journal, May 31 2026 — HIF-1α drives Pannexin-1 channel expression and ATP accumulation in ADPKD cysts** [hypothesis-generating]
- In ADPKD cyst-lining cells, HIF-1α (hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha) transcriptionally upregulates Pannexin-1 (Panx1), an ATP-release channel. Panx1 overexpression drives luminal ATP accumulation in cysts, activating purinergic P2 receptors and promoting cyst expansion. Probenecid (a Pannexin inhibitor used clinically for gout) attenuates ATP release in ADPKD cyst cells. Importantly, **human ADPKD tissue validation is included** — an unusual feature for preclinical mechanistic papers in this space.
- **Why notable:** The HIF-1α/Panx1/purinergic axis is distinct from the main V2R/cAMP, metabolic reprogramming, and polycystin-restoration approaches, and represents a potentially orthogonal entry point. Probenecid is a generic, clinically available drug. Human tissue validation increases plausibility beyond cell-line data alone.
- **Limits confidence:** In vitro and ex vivo only; no animal cyst model data reported. HIF-1α regulates hundreds of downstream targets — Panx1 may not be rate-limiting for cyst growth in the intact kidney. Probenecid has multiple pharmacological actions (uricosuric, CFTR-modulating, anti-inflammatory) making Panx1-specific attribution difficult. No eGFR or TKV outcome. ADPKD has a poor preclinical-to-clinical translation record.
- Scores: Imp 5/10 | Ev 3/10 | Nov 8/10 | Dec 3/10 | Cal 8/10

**ss_288702390 — bioRxiv preprint, May 25 2026 — CRISPRa-mediated activation of endogenous PKD1 suppresses ADPKD cellular features** [hypothesis-generating]
- A CRISPR-activation approach (catalytically dead Cas9 fused to transcriptional activators) targeting the endogenous PKD1 promoter is reported to upregulate polycystin-1 and reduce ADPKD cellular pathology in cell models. Preprint; no abstract indexed.
- **Context — polycystin-restoration toolkit expanding:** This joins the mini-CRISPRa methodology paper (Chakraborty/Yu, Mol Biol Reports May 2026, pm_42068455), VX-407 small-molecule PKD1 corrector (Phase 2a AGLOW), farabursen miR-17 inhibitor (Phase 1 completed), PYC-003 antisense RNA (Phase 1 active), and ataluren readthrough therapy (pm_41274954) — each targeting a different level of the PKD1 expression/function chain. The mechanistic diversity in this cluster is notable.
- **Limits confidence:** Preprint without peer review; no abstract accessible; cell model only; CRISPR delivery to adequate numbers of cyst-lining cells in vivo remains an unsolved challenge.
- Scores: Imp 5/10 | Ev 2/10 | Nov 7/10 | Dec 2/10 | Cal 7/10

### Biomarkers

**pm_42237252 — Khawandi et al., BMC Nephrology, June 2026 — GRADE-rated systematic review of prognostic biomarkers in ADPKD (58 studies)** [background/context]
- The most comprehensive biomarker SR to date for ADPKD: 58 studies, reviewing 33 urinary and 29 serum biomarkers assessed for prognostic utility, graded using GRADE methodology. Most studied biomarkers: osmolality, copeptin, proteinuria, MCP-1, uric acid.
- **Key finding:** No single biomarker achieves evidence strength sufficient for independent clinical decision-making beyond established risk tools (Mayo Imaging Classification, PROPKD score, Ravichandran prediction model). GRADE ratings are uniformly low-to-moderate across biomarkers, reflecting the predominantly observational, heterogeneous underlying evidence base.
- **Why useful:** This review provides the best available map of what the biomarker evidence actually shows. It is the appropriate reference point for any clinical or research discussion of individual ADPKD biomarkers and appropriately tempers single-study biomarker enthusiasm.
- **Limits confidence:** SR methodology is only as good as the underlying studies, most of which are observational and heterogeneous; no pooled effect-size meta-analysis (methodologically appropriate given heterogeneity, but limits quantitative conclusions). Does not generate new primary data.
- Scores: Imp 5/10 | Ev 5/10 | Nov 6/10 | Dec 4/10 | Cal 9/10

### Disease Management / Quality of Life

**pm_42232587 — Gittus et al., Clinical Kidney Journal, June 2026 — HRQoL in ADPKD by CKD stage and KRT modality: systematic review** [background/context]
- SR of 5 databases (January 2014–October 2024) examining health-related quality of life across different CKD stages and kidney replacement therapy (KRT) modalities in ADPKD. Quantifies HRQoL trajectory from early CKD through transplantation.
- **Limits confidence:** Heterogeneous instruments (SF-36, EQ-5D, PKD-specific scales) and heterogeneous study quality; no new primary data. Useful as synthesis and reference; does not establish whether any intervention modifies the HRQoL trajectory.
- Scores: Imp 5/10 | Ev 6/10 | Nov 5/10 | Dec 4/10 | Cal 9/10

**pm_42233447 — Mert et al., Clinical Nephrology, June 2026 — Arterial stiffness (CAVI) and choroidal vascularity index (OCT) in ADPKD** [hypothesis-generating]
- Small single-centre cross-sectional study (n=41 ADPKD with eGFR>60 vs n=40 controls). Reports elevated cardio-ankle vascular index (CAVI) and altered choroidal vascularity index (CVI, measured by optical coherence tomography) in ADPKD vs. controls.
- **Limits confidence:** Small n; cross-sectional; surrogate endpoints only; CAVI and CVI are not validated as ADPKD-specific prognostic tools; confounding by blood pressure and medication use incompletely controlled. Single centre. Cannot be used to change management.
- Scores: Imp 4/10 | Ev 3/10 | Nov 5/10 | Dec 2/10 | Cal 8/10

## Evolving Understanding

### Cardiovascular risk in ADPKD: aortic surveillance evidence accumulates

The Japanese nationwide aortic study (pm_41881380, stale) adds population-scale quantitative support to the ERA KDIGO 2025 commentary recommendation for thoracic aortic surveillance. The finding_007 evolution history now spans: the KDIGO 2025 guideline itself (ICA + thoracic aortic association), the ERA Working Group commentary (endorsing risk-stratified ICA screening and noting thoracic but not abdominal aortic elevation), and now the Japanese nationwide HR 1.76 quantification for aortic events. Together, these data establish a clear cardiovascular surveillance obligation in ADPKD that extends beyond the kidney and blood pressure, encompassing intracranial aneurysm risk and thoracic aortic risk as distinct structural concerns.

### Polycystin restoration — mechanistic diversity continues to broaden

With the CRISPRa PKD1 preprint (ss_288702390) this week, the polycystin-restoration research theme now spans: VX-407 (small molecule corrector, Phase 2a), farabursen (miR-17 inhibition, Phase 1 completed), PYC-003 (antisense RNA, Phase 1 active), mini-CRISPRa (methodology paper, Chakraborty/Yu), ataluren readthrough (PKD1 nonsense mutations), and CRISPRa transcriptional upregulation. Each entry point is mechanistically distinct. No single approach has yet demonstrated convincing Phase 2 efficacy — the first randomized clinical data are still pending (VX-407 AGLOW, farabursen Phase 2 TBD). The diversity of approaches is encouraging for the long-term odds that at least one will prove viable; it also reflects the field's incomplete understanding of which node in the polycystin-expression chain is the most effective target.

### Biomarker evidence: GRADE-rated reality check

The Khawandi SR/MA confirms that none of the proposed ADPKD biomarkers (copeptin, osmolality, MCP-1, proteinuria, uric acid, and many others) has reached evidence quality sufficient for clinical implementation beyond existing imaging-based and genetic-based risk tools. This is not a pessimistic finding — it is an accurate summary of what the literature actually supports. It also identifies the highest-priority gaps: longitudinal prospective studies with kidney failure endpoints, not just cross-sectional biomarker associations. The Ravichandran JASN 2026 prediction model (finding_021) and the serum osmolality cohort (finding_023) remain the most actionable recent additions to the biomarker landscape.

### Inflammation/innate immunity — research theme solidifies but remains preclinical

The HIF-1α/Pannexin-1/ATP finding (pm_42117913) represents a new node in the emerging inflammatory/purinergic axis of ADPKD cyst biology. Together with the W19-W20 cluster (TLR2/NF-κB, endocannabinoid/IL-6, macrophage niche dynamics), the inflammation research theme is consolidating. None of these signals has reached in vivo animal model confirmation with ADPKD-appropriate endpoints, and clinical translation remains speculative. Research prioritization value: moderate (multiple groups converging). Clinical confidence: low.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **ss_50f98c9550 (RDN-ADPKD ESH 2026 abstract, title-only).** Zero data are available. The title claims "first results from the RANDOMIZED RDN-ADPKD study" — if true, this is a step forward from the single-arm TCT 2025 data. But resist inferring direction, magnitude, or significance from a title. Treat as tracker maintenance until the abstract is accessible.

2. **pm_42117913 (HIF-1α/Pannexin-1/probenecid).** Probenecid has at least four distinct pharmacological mechanisms (uricosuric/URAT1 inhibition, CFTR modulation, Pannexin-1 inhibition, anti-inflammatory). Attributing cyst-ATP reduction specifically to Panx1 inhibition is not cleanly established by the experimental design. The human tissue data is the strongest element; the functional intervention data require more specific pharmacological tools to be compelling.

3. **pm_42237252 (GRADE-rated biomarker SR/MA).** This is appropriately calibrated. Patients or clinicians reading claims about individual ADPKD biomarkers in isolation should be directed to this SR as a reality check. Biomarker enthusiasm consistently outpaces the actual evidence in ADPKD.

4. **pm_41881380 (aortic dissection, Japanese nationwide, stale).** HR 1.76 is a moderate effect size from a large administrative database. The study is Japanese — and ADPKD epidemiology, comorbidity burden, and treatment patterns in Japan may differ from European and North American populations. The "aortic events" endpoint likely aggregates dissection and aneurysm, which have distinct pathophysiology and implications. This data point informs surveillance conversations, not screening mandates.

5. **pm_42233447 (n=41 arterial stiffness/OCT).** n=41 in a cross-sectional design cannot establish whether vascular stiffness in ADPKD is worse than expected for the same blood pressure exposure. The CAVI and CVI associations may be entirely explained by the known hypertension excess in ADPKD. Not actionable.
