## Highlights

### 1. MANGROVE Phase 2a: GLPG2737 CFTR inhibitor shows no efficacy in ADPKD — hypothesis pruned

- **What happened:** Gansevoort et al. (*Nephrology, Dialysis and Transplantation*, May 2026, pm_42201338) published the full peer-reviewed results of the MANGROVE randomized double-blind placebo-controlled Phase 2a trial (NCT04578548, 2:1 allocation). GLPG2737 150 mg/day (an oral CFTR inhibitor by Galapagos) was compared to placebo in n=66 ADPKD patients over 52 weeks. Primary endpoint: htTKV growth rate — GLPG2737 +8.2% vs placebo +9.2% (difference −0.91%, p=0.61). Key secondary endpoint: eGFR change — no significant difference (−2.31 mL/min/1.73 m², p=0.17). The trial was terminated early for lack of efficacy. A companion 52-week open-label extension was also terminated.
- **Why people may care:** The CFTR-mediated chloride secretion hypothesis had robust preclinical support: CFTR is expressed in cyst-lining epithelia, drives cyst fluid accumulation, and inhibitors reduce cyst growth in cell and animal models. MANGROVE was a well-structured Phase 2a test with a surrogate endpoint (htTKV) at an adequate dose (as validated by the preclinical program). The unambiguous negative result removes GLPG2737-class CFTR inhibition from the near-term ADPKD development pipeline and materially weakens the CFTR-channel-inhibition hypothesis as a standalone approach for slowing cyst growth in typical adult ADPKD.
- **What limits confidence:** Phase 2a, n=66 — adequately powered for surrogate endpoint detection but not for patient-important outcomes (kidney failure, dialysis). Only a single dose was tested; higher doses, different CFTR modulators, or combination regimens with tolvaptan are not excluded by this data. The negative result is specific to CFTR's channel function — CFTR's protein scaffold and HIPPO-pathway interaction roles (as in preclinical data, pm_42140432) represent mechanistically distinct angles not tested here. Note also: the trial completed in April 2023 but full results were published approximately 3 years later in May 2026, consistent with a publication lag that has been observed for negative trials.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant (negative result — prunes CFTR-channel-inhibition approach from ADPKD therapeutic space)
- **Scores:** Importance 7/10 | Evidence 7/10 | Novelty 8/10 | Decision 9/10 | Calibration 9/10

## New Papers — By Dimension

### Pharmacological / Novel Mechanisms

**pm_42176774 — Wang et al., Kidney International May 2026 — MQ232 green mamba V2R inverse agonist dissociates cAMP from disease attenuation** [hypothesis-generating]
- A disulfide-rich peptide (MQ232) from green mamba venom was characterised as a V2R inverse agonist in an orthologous Pkd1 mouse model. MQ232 shows anti-cystic efficacy comparable to tolvaptan but engages non-cAMP V2R signaling arms (Src/ERK/CRAF/Akt/RPS6/Stat3), dissociating the disease-attenuating effect from the aquaretic (polyuria-producing) side-effect profile. This implies that V2R-mediated anti-cystic effects are not mediated exclusively through cAMP suppression.
- **Limits confidence:** Preclinical, mouse model only. Venom peptides face major pharmacokinetic barriers to clinical development (stability, delivery, cost). The cAMP/aquaretic dissociation is mechanistically interesting but has not been replicated or tested in any clinical setting. ADPKD's preclinical-to-clinical translation history is poor.
- **Potential clinical relevance:** ~50% of tolvaptan patients discontinue within 12 months largely due to aquaretic side effects (polyuria, nocturia). The MQ232 data suggest the aquaretic mechanism is separable from the anti-cystic mechanism at the V2R level — this could motivate design of small molecules with modified receptor-signaling profiles, complementing the HYDROPROTECT strategy of managing aquaretic burden with HCTZ.
- Scores: Imp 5/10 | Ev 4/10 | Nov 8/10 | Dec 4/10 | Cal 9/10

**pm_42184822 — Meyer et al., British Journal of Pharmacology May 2026 — Thermal proteome profiling identifies drug repurposing candidates** [hypothesis-generating]
- Thermal proteome profiling (TPP) of ADPKD kidney cells identifies birinapant (IAP inhibitor), bardoxolone methyl (Nrf2 activator), and salicylic acid (an aspirin metabolite) as repurposing candidates, with a shared mechanism of mitochondrial ATP translocase inhibition. Salicylic acid also inhibits PTK2/FAK kinase. In vitro validation only; no in vivo ADPKD model tested.
- **Limits confidence:** Computational/proteomics screening with in vitro confirmation. Bardoxolone methyl has a concerning clinical track record (BEACON CKD trial stopped for safety; prior kidney disease development halted). Birinapant (IAP antagonist) promotes apoptosis and has complex off-target effects. In vitro thermal profiling does not predict in vivo or clinical efficacy.
- Scores: Imp 4/10 | Ev 3/10 | Nov 7/10 | Dec 3/10 | Cal 7/10

**pm_41274954 — Torban et al., Scientific Reports Nov 2025 [backfill, surfaced May 2026] — Translational readthrough therapy for PKD1 nonsense mutations** [hypothesis-generating]
- Read-through compounds (ataluren-class) induce translational readthrough of premature stop codons in PKD1, restoring polycystin-1 expression in PKD1 nonsense-mutant cell lines and reducing cystogenesis in a zebrafish ADPKD model. This approach is applicable only to patients carrying PKD1 protein-truncating nonsense mutations (~30% of PKD1 patients), not to missense, frameshift, splice-site, or PKD2 mutations.
- **Limits confidence:** In vitro and zebrafish only; no mammalian in vivo cyst model tested. Read-through therapy efficacy varies substantially across stop codon contexts. Not a clinical candidate. Adds to the polycystin-restoration toolkit (alongside VX-407 corrector for class III PKD1 variants, farabursen miR-17, mini-CRISPRa) but is the furthest from clinical application.
- Scores: Imp 5/10 | Ev 3/10 | Nov 5/10 | Dec 2/10 | Cal 8/10

**pm_41018274 — Nakajima et al., Clinical Kidney Journal Sep 2025 [backfill] — Dapagliflozin add-on to tolvaptan in ADPKD** [hypothesis-generating]
- Within-patient eGFR slope analysis in a small Japanese single-centre cohort examining the effect of adding dapagliflozin to existing tolvaptan therapy in ADPKD. Reports a potential additive eGFR-slope benefit. Single-arm, within-group design.
- **Limits confidence:** No control arm; within-group design cannot isolate dapagliflozin effect from regression-to-mean, time-on-tolvaptan effects, or other concurrent changes; small n; short follow-up. Hypothesis-generating context for the STOP-PKD trial (NCT07280585), which will provide definitive SGLT2i data, and potentially for future combination trials.
- Scores: Imp 5/10 | Ev 3/10 | Nov 5/10 | Dec 3/10 | Cal 8/10

### Dietary / GLP-1RA Preclinical

**ss_2b067bdcbc — Garimella, APS 2026 Physiology abstract [backfill] — Semaglutide reduces htTKV in high-fat-diet but not chow-diet Pkd1RC/RC mice** [hypothesis-generating]
- GLP-1RA semaglutide reduced htTKV in Pkd1RC/RC mice fed a high-fat diet but showed no significant effect in mice on a standard control diet. The effect appears diet- and metabolic-state-dependent rather than a direct anti-cystic mechanism.
- **Limits confidence:** Abstract-only APS 2026 conference disclosure; mouse model; diet-dependent effect in a preclinical model is a significant qualifier; n and dose not disclosed. Effect of GLP-1RA in lean, non-obese ADPKD cannot be inferred.
- **Relevance to the tirzepatide trial (NCT06582875, n=126):** This trial enrolls ADPKD patients with obesity. The diet-dependent preclinical signal suggests that any GLP-1RA benefit in ADPKD may be conditional on metabolic state (adiposity, high-fat dietary pattern). Pre-specification of an adiposity-stratified subgroup analysis in the tirzepatide trial protocol would strengthen interpretability of results.
- Scores: Imp 5/10 | Ev 3/10 | Nov 7/10 | Dec 4/10 | Cal 9/10

## Negative / Null Results This Week

### 1. MANGROVE Phase 2a — GLPG2737 CFTR inhibitor: no effect on htTKV or eGFR (high decision value)

This is the most decision-relevant finding of the W22 cycle. Full summary in Highlights above. The key messages for field interpretation:

**What is closed:** CFTR channel inhibition with GLPG2737 at 150 mg/day does not slow ADPKD progression in terms of TKV or eGFR over 52 weeks. The biological hypothesis that chloride secretion via CFTR is rate-limiting for cyst fluid accumulation does not appear to translate to meaningful clinical benefit at the tested dose and regimen.

**What is not closed:** (1) Whether other CFTR inhibitors at different doses would differ. (2) Whether CFTR's scaffolding/protein-interaction role (distinct from its channel function, as suggested by the CFTR-HIPPO paper pm_42140432) could be targeted differently. (3) Whether CFTR inhibition in combination with tolvaptan might show additive benefit. (4) Whether other chloride secretion channels (TMEM16A/ANO1 — see FXR/TMEM16A preclinical finding pm_41758537) represent a different path.

**Note on ADPKD therapeutic pruning:** MANGROVE joins a growing list of mechanistically plausible approaches that failed in clinical translation:
- Pravastatin (adult early ADPKD RCT, Gitomer et al.): no benefit, possible trend against
- Venglustat glucosylceramide synthase inhibition (STAGED-PKD): no TKV benefit, faster eGFR decline
- Octreotide LAR somatostatin (6-RCT meta-analysis): no kidney disease modification
- CFTR inhibition (MANGROVE): no htTKV or eGFR benefit

Each of these failures emphasises the poor preclinical-to-clinical translation record in ADPKD and the danger of over-inferring from animal and in vitro models.

## Evolving Understanding

### The therapeutic target pruning in ADPKD: a pattern worth naming

This week's MANGROVE result extends a pattern that has become visible over the last year. Multiple mechanistically plausible, preclinically supported therapeutic approaches have failed in ADPKD clinical trials. The list now includes statins (pravastatin), sphingolipid pathway inhibition (venglustat), somatostatin analogues for kidney disease (octreotide LAR), and CFTR channel inhibition (GLPG2737). In each case, robust animal model data did not translate to human benefit.

What survives this pruning? Three categories:
1. **V2R/cAMP pathway** (tolvaptan): mechanistically proven in Phase 3 RCT, but with tolerability limiting real-world effectiveness. V2R inverse agonism (MQ232) and tolvaptan tolerability management (HYDROPROTECT) address the tolerability barrier from different directions.
2. **Metabolic reprogramming** (SGLT2i, ketogenic diet, GLP-1RA): evidence ranges from promising observational to Phase 3 in-progress. STOP-PKD (dapagliflozin) and GREASE II (ketogenic diet) are the decisive pending trials. The semaglutide diet-dependent preclinical signal this week adds nuance — GLP-1RA benefit may be obesity-conditional.
3. **Precision medicine / polycystin restoration** (VX-407, farabursen, PYC-003): mutation-specific or mechanism-restorative approaches. First clinical proof-of-concept for polycystin restoration disclosed (farabursen Phase 1), but all remain early-stage.

The CFTR failure adds to the argument that broadly-expressed epithelial channels, even when mechanistically implicated in cyst fluid dynamics, may not be rate-limiting in the complex, slow-progressing human disease. Targeting a single ion transport pathway may be insufficient when multiple redundant mechanisms (TMEM16A, AQP2, NHE3, others) contribute to cyst fluid dynamics.

### GLP-1RA obesity conditionality — relevant to ongoing trial design

The semaglutide preclinical data (ss_2b067bdcbc) is a cautionary finding for the tirzepatide trial (NCT06582875). If GLP-1RA benefit in ADPKD is obesity-conditioned rather than direct anti-cystic, the enrolled patient population and subgroup analysis plan should account for this. The trial currently enrolls patients with ADPKD and obesity, so the primary analysis may be appropriately targeted — but the finding argues against extrapolating any positive result to lean ADPKD patients.

### V2R non-cAMP signaling — adds mechanistic complexity

The MQ232 finding (pm_42176774) adds evidence that the V2R mediates anti-cystic effects through pathways beyond cAMP. This has implications for how we think about tolvaptan's mechanism — the drug's benefit may not be attributable entirely to cAMP suppression and the downstream PKA pathway. If Src/ERK/Akt/Stat3 signaling contributes to disease modification, then V2R modulators that preserve some receptor engagement (partial agonists, biased agonists, inverse agonists with selective pathway profiles) might achieve anti-cystic benefit with less aquaresis.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **pm_42201338 (MANGROVE).** This is a clean, well-calibrated negative RCT publication. No skepticism adjustment on the finding. However: (a) the published abstract does not detail patient inclusion criteria (eGFR range, Mayo class) — full paper review warranted; (b) three-year publication lag (trial completed 2023, published 2026) is worth noting as a potential indicator of how negative trials reach the literature late; (c) industry-funded study (Galapagos) but a clean negative — no commercial incentive to inflate.

2. **pm_42176774 (MQ232 V2R inverse agonist).** The "dissociates cAMP from disease attenuation" framing is mechanistically exciting but should not be interpreted as near-term clinical applicability. Venom peptides are not oral drugs. The mechanistic insight is valuable for understanding V2R biology; translating it to a clinical candidate requires substantial additional work.

3. **pm_42184822 (thermal proteome profiling).** Bardoxolone methyl appeared as a "repurposing candidate" in this screen. Clinicians and patients should know that bardoxolone has had significant clinical trial failures in kidney disease (BEACON trial stopped for heart failure safety signal). Re-emergence via a new mechanism screen does not rehabilitate the drug's clinical track record.

4. **ss_2b067bdcbc (semaglutide APS abstract).** Abstract-only. The diet-dependent effect (HFD but not ConD mice) is a major qualifier that limits the scope of the positive signal. Do not interpret this as "GLP-1RA reduces cyst growth in ADPKD" without the critical caveat "in obese/high-fat-diet models only."

5. **pm_41018274 (dapagliflozin+tolvaptan combination).** Within-group design only. The apparent eGFR benefit from adding dapagliflozin is confounded by the hemodynamic dip expected from SGLT2 inhibition and regression-to-mean effects. This is not evidence of combination benefit — it is a rationale generator for the prospective RCT approach.
