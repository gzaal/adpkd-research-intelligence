## Highlights

### 1. Farabursen (miR-17 inhibitor) reports first clinical efficacy signal — pipeline milestone, not practice-changing

- **What happened:** A WCN 2026 conference abstract (WCN26-7987, *Kidney International Reports*, Yu, Garg, Bellovich, Silva, Chebib et al.) reports that farabursen (formerly RGLS8429, Regulus Therapeutics) increases urinary polycystin-1 and polycystin-2 and reduces height-adjusted total kidney volume (htTKV) growth in ADPKD patients. The Phase 1 trial (NCT05521191) is now listed as COMPLETED on ClinicalTrials.gov.
- **Why people may care:** This is the first disclosed clinical proof-of-concept that a polycystin-restoration approach can engage its target (polycystin output in urine) and also move a structural kidney endpoint (htTKV growth) in ADPKD patients. miR-17 inhibition is mechanistically distinct from V2R antagonism (tolvaptan) and from PKD1 correctors (VX-407), and this marks the first non-tolvaptan human signal on an imaging endpoint.
- **What limits confidence:** The disclosure is a conference abstract title only — no full abstract text, sample size, effect magnitudes, statistical precision, or comparator details are public. Phase 1 trials are primarily designed for safety and pharmacokinetics; efficacy signals from Phase 1 often do not replicate. The study is company-funded, likely small, single-arm with surrogate endpoints. The predecessor molecule (RGLS4326) had a CNS toxicity setback. ADPKD has a long record of preclinical and early-phase signals that did not translate.
- **Classification:** pipeline-relevant
- **Scores:** Importance 8/10 | Evidence 3/10 | Novelty 9/10 | Decision 6/10 | Calibration 8/10

### 2. Octreotide long-acting release — 6-RCT meta-analysis finds no effect on GFR or TKV

- **What happened:** A meta-analysis of 6 RCTs of octreotide LAR in ADPKD (Jin, Shi, Yu, *Frontiers in Endocrinology*, January 2026, pm_41647114) found no statistically significant effect on GFR (SMD −0.020, 95% CI −0.250 to 0.210, p=0.864) and no effect on total kidney volume (SMD −0.001, 95% CI −0.376 to 0.374, p=0.996). A borderline signal for cystic kidney volume reduction (RR 1.127, p=0.058) did not reach conventional significance. No increase in adverse events.
- **Why people may care:** Somatostatin analogues have been discussed as an ADPKD disease-modifying strategy for over 15 years. The pooled RCT evidence now shows no convincing effect on the two endpoints that matter most for kidney disease modification (GFR and TKV). This materially prunes octreotide LAR as an ADPKD kidney-disease-modifying therapy.
- **What limits confidence:** The six trials were heterogeneous in dose, duration, and population. Trial-level granularity (which specific trials contributed most weight, any subgroup with benefit) is not available from the abstract alone. The somatostatin analogue class remains clinically useful for symptomatic polycystic liver disease — this meta-analysis does not alter that role. The borderline cystic-volume signal leaves a narrow avenue open for hypothesis-generating follow-up, but current data do not support disease-modifying use.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 6/10 | Novelty 5/10 | Decision 7/10 | Calibration 9/10

### 3. Long-term PM2.5 air pollution exposure associated with faster eGFR decline in PKD

- **What happened:** A Korean National Health Information Database study (Kwag et al., *CJASN*, April 14, 2026, pm_41979893) analyzed 18,898 adults with PKD over 2016–2023. Each 1 µg/m³ increase in lag 0–1 year PM2.5 exposure was associated with a −0.09 mL/min/1.73m² change in eGFR (95% CI −0.16 to −0.01). Associations were more evident in younger adults, those with preserved baseline kidney function, males, and patients not on tolvaptan.
- **Why people may care:** This is the largest population-level observational study linking an environmental exposure to kidney function decline specifically in PKD. It reframes PKD progression as partly modifiable by non-genetic, non-pharmacological factors, and suggests patient-level conversations about air quality may be warranted, particularly for younger patients with preserved kidney function.
- **What limits confidence:** Retrospective cohort using administrative data; PKD coding in claims databases is imperfect and does not distinguish ADPKD from ARPKD or other cystic diseases; PM2.5 exposure estimated by a high-resolution model, not individual monitors; effect size is small per unit (−0.09 mL/min/1.73m² per 1 µg/m³); ADPKD-specific identifier was likely not used. Residual confounding from unmeasured lifestyle or comorbidity factors cannot be excluded. Evidence is associational only — no causal inference possible.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 5/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 8/10 | Decision 3/10 | Calibration 9/10

## Evolving Understanding

### Polycystin restoration — first non-tolvaptan clinical signal
The farabursen WCN26 abstract is the first disclosed clinical signal of an ADPKD drug that engages its target (urinary PC1/PC2 appearance is plausible pharmacodynamic evidence of miR-17 inhibition) AND shifts a structural kidney endpoint (htTKV growth) in patients. This raises the credibility of polycystin restoration as a therapeutic strategy — but does not yet establish clinical benefit. The data are abstract-level from a Phase 1 study. Phase 2 results in an adequately powered, controlled design over a longer interval are needed before this changes clinical thinking. For a motivated patient reader: this is a genuinely interesting pipeline event, not a new treatment. Expect 2–4 years before any regulatory milestone.

### Octreotide LAR — disease-modifying hypothesis no longer supported
Fifteen years of research on somatostatin analogues for ADPKD disease modification accumulates to a null meta-analysis at the class-summary level. This moves octreotide LAR from "promising repurposing candidate" to "unsupported for kidney disease modification" in our synthesis. Somatostatin analogues remain legitimately useful for severe polycystic liver disease — that is a different indication. Patients asking about octreotide for ADPKD kidney protection today should be told that the accumulated RCT evidence does not support that use.

### Pravastatin — editorial-level consolidation of negative RCT
Torres's editorial in Kidney International does not add primary data, but it consolidates the field's interpretation of the Gitomer negative RCT. The question of statins for ADPKD disease modification is now closed for adults with early disease. Statins retain their standard role for cardiovascular risk management in ADPKD patients — a separate indication with strong independent evidence.

### SGLT2 inhibitors — mosaic of small studies expanding; Phase 3 still the decisive test
Three SGLT2i items this week (Seliger empagliflozin feasibility RCT design, ElSharkawy WCN26 dapagliflozin TKV abstract, Takenaka WCN26 mechanistic review) add incremental support to the class but no definitive efficacy. STOP-PKD (n=420, Phase 3 dapagliflozin) remains the trial that will determine whether the observational signal is real. Confidence level on finding_005 unchanged (moderate).

### Environmental exposures — a new lens on modifiable progression
The PM2.5/PKD observational study introduces air pollution as an environmental modifier of ADPKD progression. The effect size is small per unit PM2.5, but urban background PM2.5 in many locations is 10–25 µg/m³, which would scale to meaningful cumulative eGFR differences. This is hypothesis-generating and does not create a clear clinical action other than general public-health advice. Worth tracking whether other cohorts replicate.

### Obesity / RBF — mechanistic candidate, not actionable
Birznieks et al. link higher BMI to lower renal blood flow in ADPKD cross-sectionally. Nowak et al. (W15 digest) previously linked low RBF to long-term kidney failure. Together these are mechanistically suggestive but the direction of causation is unproven — patients with worse ADPKD may gain weight due to reduced activity, rather than weight driving RBF reduction. No practice change.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **Farabursen abstract-only disclosure.** The WCN26 abstract is a title-level announcement. Regulus Therapeutics's prior miR-17 program (RGLS4326) hit CNS toxicity signals. The "first clinical efficacy" framing is justified but the evidence supporting it is an abstract-only Phase 1 disclosure from the sponsor. Magnitude of TKV effect, statistical precision, and safety detail are required before this can be interpreted as clinically meaningful. Flag any media coverage that characterizes this as "breakthrough" or "game-changing."

2. **Jankowska tolvaptan trajectories (pm_41597478).** This single-center retrospective n=20 paper in *Medicina* claims in its conclusion that "real-world tolvaptan efficacy may be lower than previously reported." This claim is not supported by its own design: pre/post eGFR comparison in 20 patients is underpowered, uses eGFR as the sole endpoint, labels subgroups ("beneficiaries / stable / progressors") by visual trajectory inspection (not a validated method), and is subject to regression-to-the-mean (patients frequently start tolvaptan after a rapid decline period, which then regresses). The paper should be treated as background only; it does not undermine the established tolvaptan evidence base.

3. **Japanese ketogenic 12-month interim (WCN26-7921).** Co-authored by Weimbs, who has commercial ties to Santa Barbara Nutrients (the Ren-Nu manufacturer). Single-arm likely, abstract-only, Japanese site. Extends the ketogenic line to a new population, but should not be interpreted as independent replication of efficacy. GREASE II remains the decisive ketogenic RCT.

4. **WCN26 abstract batch broadly.** A large block of this week's new items are conference abstracts without full methods. For all of them (farabursen, dapagliflozin TKV, chemical chaperones, SGLT2i pathophysiology, hyperuricemia, NLRP3, valve burden, 12-month keto), evidence strength is capped at 3/10 in this digest. Re-evaluation will occur only when full abstracts or peer-reviewed papers appear.

5. **ALDH1A1 / disulfiram / PD-L1 (pm_41864666).** The "approved drug repurposing" framing (disulfiram is approved for alcohol dependence) is appealing but entirely preclinical mouse data. Adding immunotherapy (anti-PD-L1) to a genetic cystic disease is biologically speculative. ADPKD has a poor translation record.
