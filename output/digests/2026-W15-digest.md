## Highlights

### 1. Pravastatin does not work for adult ADPKD — a definitive negative RCT

- **What happened:** A 24-month, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT (NCT03273413, Gitomer et al., *Kidney International* 2025, n=150 adults with early-stage ADPKD) found no benefit of pravastatin on htTKV growth rate (3.1%/yr placebo vs 4.3%/yr pravastatin — numerically *favoring placebo*) or measured GFR slope (−2.3 vs −1.4 mL/min/1.73m²). Renal blood flow also did not differ. This paper was surfaced by the scan and incorporated into the knowledge base this week; it was published October 2025.
- **Why people may care:** Statins — especially pravastatin — have long been discussed as potential ADPKD disease modifiers based on preclinical data and earlier pediatric observational studies (HALT PKD). This trial closes the question for adult patients.
- **What limits confidence:** The trial enrolled adults with early disease (likely Mayo 1A-1B); statin effects in more advanced disease or different disease stages cannot be formally excluded. The pediatric suggestion of benefit (earlier HALT PKD data) does NOT generalize to adults based on this trial. This does not invalidate statins for their established cardiovascular indications in ADPKD patients.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant
- **Scores:** Importance 8/10 | Evidence 8/10 | Novelty 5/10 (published Oct 2025, incorporated now) | Decision 9/10 | Calibration 9/10

### 2. PYC-003 Phase 1 trial confirmed — RNA therapy enters the ADPKD clinic

- **What happened:** A Phase 1 trial of PYC-003 (PYC Therapeutics), an antisense oligonucleotide RNA therapy for ADPKD, was confirmed as actively enrolling via an abstract at the World Congress of Nephrology 2026 (WCN26-6082, *Kidney International Reports*, April 2026). The trial enrolls approximately 116 patients (NCT06714006). No safety or efficacy data were disclosed in the abstract.
- **Why people may care:** RNA-based therapeutics represent a mechanism class entirely distinct from all existing and investigational ADPKD drugs. If an antisense approach can selectively reduce mutant PKD1 or PKD2 transcripts, it could address disease at the molecular root. The platform is already validated in other genetic diseases (e.g., inclisiran in lipid lowering, nusinersen in spinal muscular atrophy).
- **What limits confidence:** This is an abstract-only disclosure of a Phase 1 entry. No data are available. Phase 1 trials assess safety, not efficacy. ADPKD has a poor history of mechanistically exciting candidates not translating to clinical benefit.
- **Classification:** pipeline-relevant
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 1/10 (abstract only) | Novelty 9/10 | Decision 2/10 | Calibration 8/10

### 3. SGLT2 inhibitors — first real-world patient-important endpoint data in nondiabetic ADPKD

- **What happened:** A retrospective propensity-matched cohort study using the TriNetX global network (Chang et al., *Mayo Clinic Proceedings: Innovations*, March 2026, n=187 matched pairs) found SGLT2 inhibitor use was associated with significantly reduced risk of dialysis initiation in nondiabetic adults with ADPKD (HR 0.35, 95% CI 0.21–0.61; dialysis rates: 10.2% SGLT2i vs 23.0% controls). This paper was surfaced during the April 11 scan and incorporated into finding_005.
- **Why people may care:** This is the first study to report a patient-important endpoint (dialysis initiation) for SGLT2i use in nondiabetic ADPKD — prior data were limited to eGFR, blood pressure, or TKV surrogates.
- **What limits confidence:** Retrospective design. Propensity matching cannot eliminate all confounding — patients prescribed SGLT2i off-label for ADPKD may be systematically different from controls (e.g., more engaged in care, more access to specialists). HR 0.35 would be an extraordinarily large effect size for ADPKD. The Phase 3 STOP-PKD trial (n=420, dapagliflozin) is the necessary confirmation.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating (not yet practice-relevant — confounding risk is real)
- **Scores:** Importance 7/10 | Evidence 4/10 | Novelty 5/10 (March 2026 publication) | Decision 5/10 | Calibration 7/10

## Negative / Null Results This Week

**HIGH PRIORITY:**

1. **Pravastatin negative adult ADPKD RCT** (pm_41077128): A well-powered, double-blind 24-month RCT found no benefit of pravastatin on kidney volume growth or GFR decline in early-stage adult ADPKD. This trial definitively prunes pravastatin (and by extension, the statin class) from the ADPKD therapeutic space for adult disease modification. Prior pediatric data suggesting benefit (HALT PKD observational) should not be assumed to generalize to adults.

2. **Metformin null signal across 4 small pre-IMPEDE RCTs** (pm_41254555): SR/MA of 4 RCTs (n=213) shows no statistically significant benefit on eGFR or htTKV, with a real tolerability burden. This does NOT close the metformin question — IMPEDE-PKD (n=1,174) remains the pivotal test — but clinicians asking about metformin off-label today should be aware that the current evidence base is null.

## Evolving Understanding

### Pravastatin: question closed for adult ADPKD

The long-running discussion about statin repurposing for adult ADPKD disease modification can now be set aside. The well-powered negative RCT is definitive for early-stage adult disease. This is an important example of a mechanistically plausible preclinical signal that did not translate to human benefit — a recurring pattern in ADPKD drug development. Statins retain their established role in cardiovascular risk reduction and should be used for that indication when appropriate.

### Metformin: null signal so far, but the trial that matters hasn't reported

Four small RCTs are consistent in showing no clear benefit, but they were individually underpowered and had short follow-up times. IMPEDE-PKD (n=1,174, Phase 3) is the trial the field is waiting for. Pre-IMPEDE null signals should lower enthusiasm but not eliminate monitoring of this trial. A negative IMPEDE-PKD result would be a high-decision-value finding that definitively closes the metformin question.

### Ketogenic diets: metabolic feasibility confirmed; structural benefit still uncertain

The systematic review (Grammatikopoulou et al.) confirms that ketogenic dietary interventions are metabolically feasible and produce anthropometric improvements. However, the evidence for structural kidney modification (TKV reduction) remains inconsistent. The GREASE II 24-month RCT is the critical pending data point. Until those results are available, ketogenic diets should be discussed with patients as a promising intervention with a plausible mechanism, but not yet as an established treatment.

### SGLT2 inhibitors: strengthening observational signal, requires Phase 3 confirmation

The TriNetX real-world data (HR 0.35 for dialysis) is the first report of a patient-important endpoint for SGLT2i in nondiabetic ADPKD, which is meaningful. However, the extraordinary effect size and retrospective design demand caution. STOP-PKD (Phase 3, n=420, dapagliflozin) remains the trial that will determine whether this signal is real. Off-label prescribing of SGLT2i for ADPKD disease modification is not yet evidence-based, although cardiorenal indications may independently justify use in some patients.

### RNA therapy: a new modality enters the clinic

The PYC-003 Phase 1 entry is genuinely noteworthy as the first RNA antisense therapeutic to enter clinical evaluation specifically for ADPKD. Precedents in other genetic diseases (spinal muscular atrophy, hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis) have shown that this platform can be highly effective when the right target is identified. Monitoring this program is warranted, but Phase 1 is a long way from clinical applicability.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **Ren-Nu commercial study** (pm_41648752, incorporated last week): A 6.3% eGFR *increase* in ADPKD claimed by a manufacturer-sponsored, uncontrolled real-world study. This claim is extraordinary for a disease characterized by progressive decline. Without a control arm, no causal inference is possible. This study appeared this week in some research feeds; patients should be explicitly warned that this does not constitute valid evidence of ADPKD-specific benefit.

2. **RDN-ADPKD trial (TCT-577, ss_50598a97)**: Indexed in JACC this week but data presented at TCT 2025 (October 2025). This is NOT a new scientific event — it is a stale item re-indexed. Single-arm, pre-post design, commercial sponsor, surrogate endpoint (blood pressure). Hypothesis-generating at best. The re-indexing does not upgrade its evidence level.

3. **JASN correction notice (pm_41563264)**: A correction to a conference abstract about a 3-month interim analysis of ketogenic metabolic therapy in Japanese ADPKD patients. Corrections are tracker maintenance only — the correction does not alter scientific conclusions.

4. **Tolvaptan + everolimus in TSC2/PKD1 (ss_994773)**: n=2 case series from WCN26. No generalizable conclusions; rare genotype (TSC2/PKD1 contiguous gene syndrome is very different from typical ADPKD). Tracker maintenance only.

5. **PubMed investigation note**: The Gitomer group's new papers include an empagliflozin feasibility study (design/baseline characteristics, PMID 41505371) in ADPKD, which adds context to the SGLT2i story but does not provide efficacy data.
