## Highlights

### 1. Ketogenic multi-omics in ADPKD: dietary KD outperforms supplements for metabolic reprogramming — and a taurine depletion signal worth watching

- **What happened:** An APS 2026 conference abstract (ss_4a0d276c, "Ketogenic Communication in Polycystic Kidney Disease," *Physiology* May 2026) reported a comparative multi-omics study (metabolomics + proteomics) of ADPKD patients on a dietary ketogenic diet (KD) versus a ketogenic supplement diet (KSD). Of 156 serum metabolites analyzed, 18 were consistently altered on KD. Dietary KD produced superior metabolic reprogramming compared to supplemental ketones. A cautionary mechanistic signal emerged: taurine depletion was identified as a consistent metabolic consequence of KD, and combining taurine depletion with cysteamine (a candidate ADPKD therapeutic) worsened cystic index in a mouse model.
- **Why people may care:** This is the most detailed metabolomic characterization of what a ketogenic diet actually does to ADPKD patient biology. The finding that dietary KD outperforms exogenous ketone supplementation for metabolic reprogramming is directly relevant to the ongoing Godstrup Hospital BHB trial (NCT06867471), which tests supplemental ketones. The taurine-cysteamine interaction is a novel drug-diet interaction signal: if cysteamine or related compounds enter the ADPKD therapeutic space, the metabolic context of co-prescribed dietary interventions may need evaluation.
- **What limits confidence:** Abstract-only disclosure — no full methods, sample size, patient characteristics, or statistical detail are publicly indexed. The taurine + cysteamine interaction is from a mouse model, not a human study, and no ADPKD patients are currently being prescribed cysteamine specifically for ADPKD. No TKV or eGFR outcomes were reported in the abstract. The abstract is from a conference (APS annual meeting), not a peer-reviewed full publication.
- **Classification:** hypothesis-generating
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 3/10 | Novelty 8/10 | Decision 4/10 | Calibration 8/10

### 2. First ADPKD-specific patient-reported outcome measure for tolvaptan polyuria burden validated (TIPS questionnaire, n=152, UMCG)

- **What happened:** Bais, Meijer et al. (*Nephrology Dialysis Transplantation*, May 11 2026, pm_42114268) report the development and validation of the TIPS questionnaire — the **Tolvaptan Interference with Patients' life Scale** — at the University Medical Center Groningen, in 152 ADPKD patients receiving tolvaptan. TIPS is the first validated ADPKD-specific instrument designed to quantify the polyuria burden of tolvaptan treatment.
- **Why people may care:** Until now, no standardized, disease-specific tool existed to measure how much tolvaptan's aquaretic side effects affect quality of life. This matters because approximately 50% of patients discontinue tolvaptan within 12 months (Marshall et al., 2026), predominantly citing aquaretic burden, yet "aquaretic burden" has never been operationally defined or scored in a validated way. TIPS provides infrastructure for: (1) systematic measurement in clinical practice and shared decision-making, (2) identifying patients at highest risk of discontinuation, and (3) an outcome measure for tolerability interventions like HYDROPROTECT (NCT05373264).
- **What limits confidence:** Development and initial validation at a single centre (UMCG Groningen); psychometric properties in other populations and languages are not yet established; the questionnaire assesses burden but does not predict clinical outcomes (such as discontinuation or adherence). External validation in non-European cohorts is needed before broad adoption.
- **Classification:** background/context (with moderate clinical-research relevance)
- **Scores:** Importance 5/10 | Evidence 6/10 | Novelty 7/10 | Decision 4/10 | Calibration 9/10

## Evolving Understanding

### Ketogenic diets — the metabolic footprint is specific to full dietary change, not supplementation

The APS 2026 abstract (ss_4a0d276c) is the first systematic metabolomic comparison between dietary KD and supplemental KSD in ADPKD patients. The observation that 18 of 156 metabolites shift consistently on KD but that dietary KD substantially outperforms KSD for metabolic reprogramming has a direct implication: **the ongoing Godstrup BHB supplementation trial (NCT06867471) and the dietary ketogenic trials (GREASE II, NCT06325644) are testing different biological interventions, not simply different delivery routes to the same metabolic state.** A positive exogenous ketone trial would not validate a dietary KD, and a positive dietary KD trial would not validate exogenous ketones.

The taurine depletion finding adds a new variable to monitor in future trials. Taurine is a sulfur-containing amino acid with multiple roles in kidney and cardiac biology. Whether chronic KD in ADPKD patients produces clinically relevant taurine depletion, and whether this interacts with any co-prescribed medications, needs prospective assessment. The cysteamine signal is a mouse mechanistic observation — it does not imply any current risk for ADPKD patients — but it warrants a data-safety-monitoring alert for any future trial combining dietary KD with thiol-based compounds.

### Tolvaptan tolerability — the measurement infrastructure is now available

The TIPS questionnaire (pm_42114268) closes a gap that has existed since tolvaptan was approved: there was no disease-specific, validated way to quantify how much the aquaretic side effects affect patients' daily lives. The practical relevance is not just for clinical trials. Shared decision-making conversations — where patients and physicians weigh the kidney-protective benefit of tolvaptan against its lifestyle impact — have until now relied on informal descriptions of polyuria burden. The TIPS questionnaire could eventually standardize this conversation and help identify patients who are suffering enough from tolerability that dose modification or the HYDROPROTECT HCTZ adjuvant approach (NCT05373264) should be prioritized.

### Preclinical mechanism activity — three separate papers, no clinical direction change

The RASSF4/PKD2 interaction (pm_42141135), CFTR-HIPPO/TAZ/YAP co-assembly (pm_42140432), and human kidney tubuloid platform (pm_42115942) all represent incremental additions to the basic science infrastructure. None changes the clinical pipeline outlook. The CFTR-HIPPO paper is notable because it connects two independently studied targets (CFTR and Hippo signalling) via a physical interaction mediated through PKD1 biology, which could eventually inform combination approaches. The tubuloid platform matters because patient-derived human models are better suited than rodent models for studying compound-specific effects — particularly relevant for precision medicine approaches like VX-407 where mutation-specific responses need to be tested.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **ss_4a0d276c (APS 2026 ketogenic multi-omics, Physiology May 2026).** Conference abstract: no methods, sample size, or patient characteristics in indexed metadata. The "18 of 156 metabolites consistently altered" statement cannot be evaluated without knowing the correction threshold, effect sizes, or between-patient variability. The taurine-cysteamine finding is a mouse model observation. Do not generalize to human risk without replication.

2. **pm_42140432 (CFTR-HIPPO, J Biol Chem).** The connection between CFTR, HIPPO/TAZ/YAP, and PKD1 biology is mechanistically interesting but builds on multiple layers of inference (mouse kidney + co-IP + VX-809). Resist the temptation to interpret this as evidence that VX-809 or other CFTR correctors will work in ADPKD via a HIPPO-mediated mechanism. The preclinical-to-clinical translation track record in ADPKD is poor.

3. **pm_42105310 (CTR/LVH, JMRI).** No abstract available; importance and evidence scores are provisional. The concept of using a cardiac surrogate derived from a kidney imaging study is appealing from a workflow perspective but requires rigorous validation against cardiac MRI gold-standard before any screening recommendation can be made.

4. **General preclinical-to-clinical caveat for this week's mechanistic batch.** RASSF4, CFTR-HIPPO, and HDAC6 are all legitimate basic-science findings. None is close to clinical application. Any patient-facing communication about "new discoveries in ADPKD" this week should note that these are laboratory findings, not pipeline candidates.
