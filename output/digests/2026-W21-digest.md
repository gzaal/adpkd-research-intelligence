## Highlights

### 1. Pregnancy does not appear to accelerate ADPKD progression — but the evidence has real limits

- **What happened:** Lapierre-Nguyen et al. (*Journal of the American Society of Nephrology*, May 2026, pm_42149684) published a secondary analysis of HALT-PKD Study A and B data in 455 women. Having 1–2 or 3+ prior pregnancies was not significantly associated with eGFR slope, total kidney volume change, or a composite kidney failure outcome after multivariable adjustment. A propensity-matched in-study pregnancy subgroup analysis (n=13 pregnant, matched 1:4) also showed a null result: eGFR slope difference Beta −0.14 mL/min/1.73m²/yr (95% CI −2.72 to +2.44).
- **Why people may care:** Pregnancy in ADPKD has historically generated clinical concern based on small case series and observational reports of worsening proteinuria and hypertension during gestation. This HALT-PKD secondary analysis is the largest longitudinal dataset to address this question with both prior-pregnancy history and prospective kidney outcomes. A null result is reassuring for patients of childbearing age navigating reproductive decisions.
- **What limits confidence:** The in-study pregnancy arm n=13 is extremely small — the 95% confidence interval (−2.72 to +2.44 mL/min/1.73m²/yr) spans a range that is clinically indistinguishable from "no effect" to "meaningful harm," so this analysis cannot rule out a clinically relevant negative effect. HALT-PKD enrolled predominantly Caucasian, non-diabetic patients at US academic centres with relatively preserved eGFR (Study A: eGFR 60–89), so the reassurance may not extend to patients with more advanced CKD or greater comorbidity. Critically, this analysis addresses kidney disease progression only — it does not evaluate obstetric outcomes (preeclampsia, gestational hypertension, preterm birth) that are also relevant to counselling. This is a secondary analysis of a trial not designed for this question.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant (counselling)
- **Scores:** Importance 8/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 8/10 | Decision 8/10 | Calibration 7/10

### 2. Hypertension is common, frequently masked, and associated with cardiac remodelling in children with ADPKD

- **What happened:** Heming et al. (*Pediatric Nephrology*, May 19 2026, pm_42151644) reported a single-centre cross-sectional study of 116 children (<18 years) with ADPKD. Overt hypertension was present in 12% (n=14); an additional 7% (n=8) had masked hypertension detected on ambulatory BP monitoring, giving a combined elevated-BP prevalence of 19%. Children with high BP (n=30, 26%) had higher cyst burden than normotensives (56% vs 34%, p=0.036), higher rates of left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH: 16.6% vs 4.6%, p=0.034), and greater relative LV wall thickness (0.34 vs 0.29, p<0.001).
- **Why people may care:** Masked hypertension — elevated BP not detectable on standard clinic measurement — is a documented but easily overlooked risk in children with ADPKD. Its detection requires 24-hour ambulatory BP monitoring (ABPM), which is not universally performed. The association between elevated BP and both higher cyst burden and LVH in the same cross-sectional cohort suggests that children with uncontrolled BP face compounding structural risks: faster kidney growth and early cardiac damage. This reinforces KDIGO 2025 guidance recommending BP surveillance in all children with ADPKD, including ABPM.
- **What limits confidence:** Single-centre cross-sectional design — cannot establish whether elevated BP precedes cyst growth or vice versa; both associations could reflect a shared driver (e.g., RAAS activation) rather than a BP-cyst causal pathway. n=116 is modest; LVH was defined by echocardiographic criteria that have not been validated specifically in ADPKD children; the tertiary referral centre likely overestimates the prevalence of BP and cardiac complications in the broader pediatric ADPKD population.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant (pediatric monitoring)
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 7/10 | Decision 6/10 | Calibration 8/10

## New Papers — By Dimension

### Pharmacological / Preclinical (APS 2026 Conference Abstracts)

All items in this section are **abstract-only disclosures from the American Physiological Society Annual Meeting (APS 2026)**. No full methods, complete sample sizes, or peer-reviewed publications are available. All are hypothesis-generating. ADPKD's preclinical-to-clinical translation record is poor; none of these findings should be interpreted as evidence of clinical utility.

**ss_2aff4edc — APS 2026, Physiology — Netrin-1 sex dimorphism in ADPKD mice** [hypothesis-generating]
- Male Pkd1RC/RC mice had significantly greater renal netrin-1 than females (sex interaction p<0.0001), with associated higher cyst growth, BUN, and cell proliferation. Netrin-1 neutralizing antibody (10 mg/kg/48h, 4 weeks; n=4–5/group) reduced cyst growth and improved kidney function in both sexes, with more pronounced effects in males (sex × treatment interaction p<0.002). First identification of netrin-1 as a candidate sex-specific amplifier of disease severity in a preclinical ADPKD model.
- Limits confidence: abstract-only; n=4–5/group; mouse model; no human data; sex-specific mechanisms in ADPKD are understudied and this single finding cannot establish whether netrin-1 mediates the clinical sex difference in ADPKD progression.
- Scores: Imp 5/10 | Ev 3/10 | Nov 7/10 | Dec 3/10 | Cal 8/10

**ss_6ab8e58c — APS 2026, Physiology — Lithium carbonate NEGATIVE in Pkd1RC/RC mice: reduces kidney volume but worsens cystic index in males** [hypothesis-generating; negative preclinical result]
- Li₂CO₃ (0.2% dietary, 8 weeks) reduced kidney volume on ultrasound in both sexes of Pkd1RC/RC mice and reduced total kidney mass in males (p=0.0178). However, it worsened cystic index (cystic space proportion) in males (p=0.0262) and reduced tubular min fractal dimension in males. Tissue injury, fibrosis, cyst quantity, and cross-sectional cyst area were not improved. The dissociation between reduced kidney volume and worsened cystic index suggests hemodynamic rather than structural benefit.
- Limits confidence: abstract-only; mouse model; sex-specific effects may not translate to humans; lithium has known renal toxicity in humans that would complicate clinical use; these are 8-week murine results.
- Decision value: moderate — this is a cautionary preclinical result that prunes lithium as a simple cAMP-inhibiting tolvaptan alternative.
- Scores: Imp 4/10 | Ev 3/10 | Nov 6/10 | Dec 4/10 | Cal 8/10

**ss_41fcce1a — APS 2026, Physiology — Metformin + cinacalcet synergistic in human 3D ADPKD cells** [hypothesis-generating]
- Combined metformin (200 µM) + cinacalcet (1 nM, 3 days) significantly reduced forskolin-induced cyst growth in human 3D ADPKD cyst-lining epithelial cells vs. either drug alone. Synergistic suppression of S6 ribosomal protein phosphorylation (mTORC1 marker). Dual treatment fully restored mitochondrial ATP production and oxidative phosphorylation — improvements beyond single-drug effects. Both drugs are clinically available.
- Limits confidence: in vitro only, no animal or patient data; forskolin-induced cyst assay is a standard but simplified model; in vitro synergy does not predict clinical efficacy or tolerability.
- Scores: Imp 4/10 | Ev 2/10 | Nov 7/10 | Dec 3/10 | Cal 8/10

**pm_42149896 — Wang et al., ACS Chemical Biology — miR-21 precursor degradation via TGP-21-RiboTAC in PKD cell lines** [hypothesis-generating]
- TGP-21-RiboTAC, a small-molecule RNA-targeted degrader, selectively degrades pre-miR-21 in polycystic kidney cell lines, leading to derepression of PDCD4 and PPARα and inhibition of proliferation. In vitro only; no in vivo cyst model tested. Extends the miRNA-targeting rationale beyond miR-17 (farabursen) to miR-21. PPARα relevance connects with the selective PPARα modulator WCN26-6644 abstract.
- Limits confidence: in vitro PKD cell line; no in vivo data; RNA-targeted degraders face delivery challenges in kidney; no relationship to any current clinical program.
- Scores: Imp 4/10 | Ev 3/10 | Nov 7/10 | Dec 2/10 | Cal 8/10

### Management / Pain

**ss_5045e01724 + ss_59e85678 — APS 2026, Physiology — Renal sensory neuron excitability in PKD mice** [hypothesis-generating]
- Two companion APS 2026 abstracts from the same group. (1) Vasopressin drives increased excitability in isolated renal sensory neurons from PKD mice — the V2R pathway, already known as a cyst-growth driver, may also amplify renal pain signals. (2) Renal sensory neuron excitability is increased in PKD models and is shown to occur independently of renal cysticity, suggesting a neural mechanism of pain that is not solely a consequence of physical cyst pressure.
- Limits confidence: abstract-only; mouse model; renal pain mechanisms in humans are incompletely understood; translational relevance uncertain.
- Clinical relevance: kidney pain affects quality of life in ~60% of ADPKD patients; mechanistic understanding of pain pathways is underdeveloped. These early signals support continued investigation, including the Somatosensory Phenotyping of ADPKD study (NCT06970028, KU Leuven).
- Scores: Imp 4/10 | Ev 2/10 | Nov 7/10 | Dec 3/10 | Cal 8/10

### Management / Renal Replacement Therapy

**pm_41023960 — Jefferis et al., BMC Nephrology Sep 2025 [backfill; surfaced this cycle]** [background/context]
- ANZDATA Registry retrospective cohort: n=63,875 patients commencing KRT in Australia/New Zealand 1963–2020, including n=2,466 ADPKD. ADPKD vs non-ADPKD: all-cause mortality on dialysis aHR 0.71 (95% CI 0.67–0.75); post-transplant mortality aHR 0.96 (not significantly different); kidney allograft survival aHR 0.87 (slightly better in ADPKD). Cardiovascular death proportions similar (27.5% vs 27.6%). This is the largest registry-based comparison of ADPKD KRT outcomes published.
- Limits confidence: retrospective registry data spanning 60 years; case-mix confounding (ADPKD patients commencing KRT are typically younger with fewer comorbidities than other KRT populations) is the dominant limitation and likely explains the survival advantage; no adjustment for KRT initiation age or comorbidity index. Published Sep 2025, surfaced in this scan cycle — not new this week.
- Clinical implication: ADPKD patients can be counselled that long-term KRT outcomes are generally favourable relative to other causes of kidney failure, with the caveat that this reflects disease-specific case-mix rather than any protective mechanism. The similar cardiovascular death proportion confirms that cardiovascular risk is not reduced in ADPKD, consistent with known comorbidities (hypertension, LVH, intracranial aneurysm, aortic dilation).
- Scores: Imp 5/10 | Ev 5/10 | Nov 4/10 | Dec 4/10 | Cal 9/10

## Negative / Null Results This Week

### 1. Pregnancy and ADPKD kidney progression — null result (high decision value for counselling)

The HALT-PKD secondary analysis (pm_42149684) found no significant association between prior or in-study pregnancy and ADPKD progression outcomes. This is the most rigorous dataset to date addressing this question. The null result should materially reduce — but not eliminate — clinical concern about pregnancy-driven ADPKD acceleration. Remaining uncertainty: the in-study n=13 arm is underpowered; obstetric complications (preeclampsia, gestational hypertension) are not addressed; advanced-CKD patients are not well represented. Clinicians and patients should use this finding in counselling while acknowledging its limits.

### 2. Lithium carbonate — negative preclinical result (prunes a proposed tolvaptan alternative)

The APS 2026 abstract (ss_6ab8e58c) reports that lithium carbonate reduced kidney volume but worsened cystic index in male Pkd1RC/RC mice. While lithium was never a clinical candidate for ADPKD (its renal toxicity profile makes it unsuitable), this result is relevant because it exposes a measurement risk: **kidney volume reduction without structural improvement is not proof of disease modification**. This caution applies broadly — investigators and patients should be alert to interventions that reduce kidney volume on imaging without demonstrating cyst-level benefit.

## Evolving Understanding

### Pregnancy counselling — the reassurance is real but imprecise

The HALT-PKD pregnancy null result (pm_42149684) is the most practice-informative finding of this digest cycle. Clinically, this shifts the prior towards reassurance, but the prior was never strongly supportive of harm either — it was based on observational case series with substantial confounding. What the HALT-PKD secondary analysis adds is rigour: longitudinal outcomes data from a well-characterized cohort, with multivariable adjustment.

The residual uncertainty is genuine and clinicians should be specific about it when counselling patients. The null result is most reliable for: (1) the question of whether prior pregnancies are associated with lifetime ADPKD progression rate (reasonably well-powered in this cohort), and (2) whether patients with early-stage, well-controlled ADPKD face measurable accelerated progression from pregnancy. It is least informative about: (1) patients with eGFR <30, (2) patients with severe hypertension at baseline, (3) obstetric outcomes themselves, and (4) whether multiple sequential pregnancies in combination with other risk factors create an additive burden. A joint ADPKD-obstetric clinic model remains the appropriate standard for managing these patients.

### Pediatric ADPKD surveillance — masked hypertension deserves more systematic attention

The Heming et al. cross-sectional data (pm_42151644) reinforces a message that has been building across multiple pediatric ADPKD studies this year: hypertension in children with ADPKD is both more common than routine clinic measurement suggests and associated with meaningful target-organ damage. The 7% masked hypertension rate in this cohort means that standard BP measurement during a clinic appointment would miss approximately 1 in 3 hypertensive children. The association with LVH adds urgency — cardiac remodelling in childhood ADPKD is preventable if identified and treated early.

The Seeman et al. macroscopic hematuria series (pm_42098312; incorporated W19) and the Heming et al. BP/cardiac paper together make a consistent case: both BP control and cardiovascular monitoring need to be more systematically structured in pediatric ADPKD surveillance, and ABPM should be standard rather than optional.

### Preclinical sex dimorphism — an emerging research thread

Three preclinical signals this cycle involve sex-specific biology in ADPKD: (1) netrin-1 is higher in male Pkd1RC/RC mice with greater cystic growth (ss_2aff4edc), (2) lithium's negative cystic-index effect was worse in males (ss_6ab8e58c), and (3) KRM niche-filling deficit was sex-specific in female cystic mice (pm_42092347, incorporated W19). The convergence of sex-specific signals across different mechanistic axes (guidance molecules, cAMP-targeting agents, innate immunity) suggests that male and female ADPKD may have partially different molecular drivers of severity — which could have implications for future trial stratification. No clinical action is justified based on preclinical data alone, but this thread warrants systematic investigation in human cohorts.

## Skepticism Notes

1. **pm_42149684 (pregnancy, JASN).** The headline "pregnancy was not associated with ADPKD progression" is justified for the prior-pregnancy multivariable analysis. It is imprecise for the in-study pregnancy subgroup (n=13, wide CI). A patient reading this study should understand: "We did not find evidence that pregnancy accelerated kidney disease in this group, but the study was too small during active pregnancy to rule out a moderate effect."

2. **pm_42151644 (pediatric BP, Pediatric Nephrology).** Cross-sectional design: the association between elevated BP and cyst burden could be interpreted in either direction (worse cysts → more RAAS activation → hypertension, rather than hypertension → faster cyst growth). Single-centre tertiary referral centre likely enriches for symptomatic and more-advanced pediatric ADPKD. The 12% overt hypertension prevalence is higher than some earlier series and may not generalise broadly.

3. **APS 2026 abstract batch (ss_2aff4edc, ss_6ab8e58c, ss_41fcce1a, ss_5045e01724, ss_59e85678).** All are conference abstracts without full methods, sample sizes, or peer review. Preclinical mouse models. ADPKD's preclinical-to-clinical translation record is poor. None of these findings should be communicated to patients as "new discoveries" until published with full data.

4. **pm_41023960 (ANZDATA).** The lower all-cause dialysis mortality in ADPKD reflects case-mix advantage, not a disease-protective mechanism. ADPKD patients beginning dialysis are younger and have fewer comorbidities. This finding should not be used to frame ADPKD as a "more benign" condition before KRT.
