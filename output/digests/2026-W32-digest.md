## Highlights

*(Selected for decision usefulness, not excitement — none of the three below should change practice today.)*

### 1. Large US registry: peritoneal dialysis associated with lower mortality and higher transplant rate than hemodialysis in ADPKD — but this is confounded observational evidence, not proof of a PD benefit

- **What happened:** A USRDS registry analysis of 53,651 incident ADPKD end-stage-kidney-disease patients (age ≥30, 2000–2021, followed to 2023) found peritoneal dialysis (PD, used by 26% of patients) associated with lower mortality (adjusted HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.80–0.88) and higher likelihood of kidney transplantation (adjusted HR 1.12, 95% CI 1.08–1.16) compared with hemodialysis, with both associations strengthening over time on therapy.
- **Why people may care:** ADPKD patients are often considered good PD candidates (young, low comorbidity) but clinicians sometimes hesitate over concerns about mechanical complications from cyst burden. This is the largest ADPKD-specific PD-vs-HD outcomes dataset reported to date and runs counter to that hesitancy.
- **What limits confidence:** Retrospective, non-randomized; PD-selected patients were systematically younger, more often female, and had lower comorbid burden. Covariate-adjusted Cox models cannot rule out unmeasured selection (e.g., abdominal cyst burden, prior abdominal surgery, capacity for self-care dialysis) that plausibly drives both PD candidacy and survival independent of any causal PD effect. The published conclusion ("PD is a safe and advantageous dialysis option... associated with longer patient survival") states this more causally than the design supports.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant (informs counseling; does not settle the causal question)
- **Scores:** Importance 7/10 | Evidence 5/10 | Novelty 7/10 | Decision 6/10 | Calibration 6/10

### 2. Largest reported ADPKD cyst-sclerotherapy series shows durable volume reduction — but its own data show declining kidney function, and the paper's "reduced renal function decline" claim is not supported by a comparator

- **What happened:** A retrospective single-center series (306 patients, 567 cysts treated with sodium tetradecyl sulfate sclerotherapy, 2014–2022) reported 99% of cysts showed volume reduction at first follow-up (mean 162 days) and continued reduction in 80% at long-term follow-up (mean 1,359 days), with mean TKV falling 371 cm³. A companion porcine bladder model showed epithelial denudation without deep mural injury, offering a plausible mechanism.
- **Why people may care:** This is the largest ADPKD cyst-sclerotherapy dataset published to date and could inform interventional management of symptomatic mass effect.
- **What limits confidence:** Single-arm, retrospective, no comparator or untreated benchmark. Critically, the same cohort's own long-term data (mean 1,460-day follow-up) show serum creatinine *increased* and eGFR *decreased* by 8.6 mL/min/1.73m² — kidney function declined in absolute terms during the study. The paper's conclusion that sclerotherapy "is associated with reduced renal function decline" has no comparator or expected-trajectory benchmark to support that framing in the available data — a clear headline-vs-evidence mismatch. This is a mass-effect symptom-management procedure; it should not be read as disease-modifying.
- **Classification:** practice-relevant (symptomatic management only) — flagged for skepticism
- **Scores:** Importance 5/10 | Evidence 3/10 | Novelty 7/10 | Decision 4/10 | Calibration 4/10

### 3. VX-407 (AGLOW) Phase 2a enrollment closes — pipeline-progression milestone, no data yet

- **What happened:** Vertex's Phase 2a AGLOW trial of the PKD1-corrector VX-407 (NCT07161037) moved from RECRUITING to ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING, with enrollment finalized at 26 participants (up from an estimated 24). Primary completion remains estimated 2027-07-22. (Already alerted 2026-08-04.)
- **Why people may care:** VX-407 is a first-in-class PKD1-targeted corrector; enrollment closure is a concrete, if routine, step toward the trial's first readout.
- **What limits confidence:** Registry status/enrollment change only — no efficacy, safety, or interim data. Enrollment closure says nothing about eventual outcome.
- **Classification:** pipeline-relevant
- **Scores:** Importance 6/10 | Evidence N/A (registry event) | Novelty 9/10 | Decision 5/10 | Calibration 9/10

## Evolving Understanding

**1. SGLT2 inhibitors (finding_005) — nuance added, confidence unchanged (MODERATE).** A second independent systematic review sharpens rather than strengthens the picture: functional benefits (BP, proteinuria, metabolic markers) are more consistently supported across the literature than structural (TKV) benefit, which remains "slight or inconsistent." This refines what "promising" should mean for this drug class pending STOP-PKD/EMPA-PKD/Seliger pilot data.

**2. Novel investigational pipeline (finding_004) — a quiet week that looked busier than it was.** Two of this cycle's "new" pipeline items (combination siRNA, collagen-MRI) turned out to be re-indexed PubMed disclosures of preprints already incorporated at W31 — the second consecutive cycle this deduplication gap has recurred (see Skepticism Notes #2). Genuine additions were modest: an osteopontin single-cell atlas (mouse-only, explicitly "modest" effect) and an ~8-month-old Ogt-deletion preprint with a notably large preclinical survival signal that warrants caution before assuming translatability. Confidence remains MODERATE.

**3. ADPKD prognostic-tool landscape (finding_021) — generalizability evidence extended, confidence unchanged (MODERATE).** The first external validation of the Mayo Clinic tool outside its original derivation cohorts (Southeast Asia, n=50) is a preliminary but welcome data point; a companion review maps the field's shift toward multimodal (imaging + genetic + AI) approaches without yet delivering one.

**4. Imaging biomarkers (finding_008) — a genuinely novel functional-imaging concept enters the tracker.** ²³Na MRI cyst-phenotyping (ppr_1290523) is mechanistically interesting — V2R expression tracks a specific sodium-handling cyst phenotype — but remains exploratory (n=20, no treatment-response data) and does not change TKV's standing as the field's validated surrogate.

**5. Dialysis modality (finding_029, new) and cyst sclerotherapy (finding_030, new) — both LOW confidence, both practice-adjacent.** The PD-vs-HD registry finding is reassuring but confounded; the sclerotherapy series is the largest to date for symptom management but its own renal-function claim is not supported by its own data (see Highlight 2).

## Skepticism Notes

1. **pm_42530143 (β-hydroxybutyrate "renoprotective hormone" review, Weimbs lab).** Authored by the same lab running the KETO-ADPKD/GREASE trials tracked in finding_002, with an established commercial and research interest in ketogenic/BHB interventions. No new primary data. The title's "renoprotective hormone" framing overstates what is proven in humans. Claim calibration capped at 5/10.
2. **pm_42538939 and pm_42539110 — re-indexed duplicates, not new science, with missed conflicts of interest.** Both are PubMed full-text disclosures of preprints (ppr_1282997, ppr_1283036) already incorporated in the W31 digest — the *second consecutive cycle* this scan-pipeline deduplication gap has recurred (flagged at W31 for a different pair of papers) and worth fixing before the next cycle. Full-text review (not available to the scan pass, which only sees abstracts/snippets) also surfaced two conflicts of interest the scan scored "None apparent": pm_42538939's authors include three co-founders of Silver Spur Therapeutics, Inc., which is commercializing the siRNA-delivery platform studied; pm_42539110's senior author holds equity and patents in InLighta Biosciences, which commercializes the collagen-MRI contrast-agent platform studied. Neither conflict disqualifies the preclinical work, but both should travel with the finding.
3. **pm_42530878 (cyst sclerotherapy) — a headline-vs-evidence mismatch.** See Highlight 2. The paper's conclusion claims an association with "reduced renal function decline," but its own data show an absolute eGFR decline over long-term follow-up, with no comparator or expected-trajectory benchmark offered to support the "reduced" framing. Treat the volume-reduction findings as credible (99% response, plausible mechanism from the porcine model) and the renal-function claim as unsupported by the evidence presented.
4. **pm_42536420 (PD vs. HD) — causal language ahead of design.** The paper's own conclusion ("PD is a safe and advantageous dialysis option... associated with longer patient survival") reads more causally than a retrospective, non-randomized, covariate-adjusted registry analysis can support. Confounding by indication (healthier, younger, more capable patients selected for PD) is the central alternative explanation and cannot be excluded by the adjustments made.
5. **Data-hygiene correction: the W31 digest's "703 total papers" figure was an arithmetic error.** Cross-checking against `data/papers.json.bak` (dated 2026-07-30, pre-W31) shows the correct pre-W31 total was 614 (234 incorporated + 270 new + 86 pending_synthesis + 24 missing-status). W31 incorporated 89 of those, which does not change the total — but the W31 digest reported 703, which is exactly 614+89, suggesting the cycle's incorporated-count was mistakenly added to the total rather than being a subset of it. Corrected total as of this cycle: 640 (614 + 26 papers added by the 08-04/08-06/08-08 scans). No downstream findings were affected by this arithmetic error — it was a bookkeeping display issue only.
