# ADPKD Research Alert — 2026-04-21

## ALERT — Pipeline/Regulatory — CJASN modeling paper provides first quantitative TKV-to-eGFR framework for ADPKD accelerated drug approval (Yu et al.)

**What happened:** Yu et al. (Clin J Am Soc Nephrol, published Feb 13, 2026) built an intraindividual mixed-effects model from CRISP and HALT-PKD Study A data relating log(TKV) to eGFR stratified by Mayo Imaging Class, estimating that within classes 1C-1E, each 1 percentage-point/year reduction in TKV growth rate corresponds to an eGFR slope improvement of ~0.40-0.52 mL/min/1.73m²/year. The authors explicitly position this as a framework to define the magnitude of TKV treatment effect needed to support FDA accelerated approval of ADPKD drugs.

**Why people may care:** This directly addresses the regulatory science gap that has constrained ADPKD drug development since TKV was formally accepted as a "reasonably likely" surrogate endpoint. Clear quantitative targets reshape trial design and go/no-go calls for the active Phase 2/3 pipeline (VX-407, ABBV-CLS-628, PYC-003, JMKX003142, farabursen, CAM2029, tamibarotene) and strengthens the rationale for accelerated-approval filings when a compound hits a meaningful TKV signal.

**What limits confidence:** The model is built on observational CRISP and HALT-PKD A data with intraindividual slopes, not randomized treatment effects; generalizability to Mayo class 2 (typical atypical/non-PKD1), very advanced 1E patients, and non-cAMP mechanisms is not yet validated; the translation ratio is an estimate with uncertainty bounds that the full paper must be read to characterize. It does not yet constitute FDA acceptance of any specific surrogate threshold.

**Classification:** pipeline-relevant

**Study design:** Retrospective intraindividual modeling of CRISP + HALT-PKD Study A registry data using linear mixed-effects models with log(TKV) fixed effect by Mayo Imaging Class stratum
**Publication event:** full-paper-published
**Endpoint:** surrogate — TKV growth rate ↔ eGFR slope translation
**Comparator:** baseline-only (intraindividual modeling)
**Result:** positive (established a quantitative relationship)
**Skepticism flags:** Retrospective / observational registry analysis; surrogate endpoint only; dependent on historical cohort representativeness; generalization to non-cAMP mechanisms untested

## Additional notable items this week (below alert threshold)

### Marshall et al. — Real-world tolvaptan propensity-weighted retrospective cohort (Am J Nephrol, Feb 2026)

**What happened:** Single-centre UK tertiary cohort of 327 ADPKD patients (119 tolvaptan-treated vs 208 untreated) with mean 61.4-month follow-up. After propensity overlap weighting, tolvaptan was associated with a non-significant +0.45 mL/min/1.73m²/year slowing in chronic eGFR slope (95% CI -0.37 to +1.18). 52.1% of tolvaptan patients discontinued within 12 months, primarily due to aquaretic side effects.

**Why people may care:** Reinforces that real-world tolvaptan effectiveness appears smaller than TEMPO 3:4 point estimates, largely driven by early discontinuation; relevant for counseling and implementation decisions.

**What limits confidence:** Single-centre retrospective design, confounding by indication, informative censoring, and survivor bias in long-term subgroups; primary CI includes null.

### Beshr et al. — Somatostatin analogs in polycystic liver disease: meta-analysis with trial sequential analysis (ILIVER, Jan 2026)

**What happened:** RCT meta-analysis evaluating somatostatin analogs for PLD with total liver volume, TKV, and eGFR endpoints plus trial sequential analysis.

**Why people may care:** Complements the Jin et al octreotide LAR ADPKD-focused meta (pm_41647114 — negative for GFR/TKV) by providing a PLD-focused synthesis relevant for symptomatic hepatomegaly cases and for the ongoing CAM2029 Phase 2/3 program (NCT05281328).

**What limits confidence:** Full abstract-level results/conclusions were not available in fetched metadata; small underlying RCT base; surrogate (volume) rather than symptom-based endpoints.
