# ADPKD Scan Alert — 2026-06-18

## ALERT — Pipeline-relevant — Probenecid blunts tolvaptan-induced polyuria in ADPKD mice and reduced urine volume/nocturia in a Phase 2 human trial

**What happened:** A *Journal of Clinical Investigation* translational study described a vasopressin-independent urate–AMPK–AQP2 water-reabsorption pathway and showed that the ABCG2 inhibitor probenecid attenuated tolvaptan-induced polyuria in wild-type and Pkd1RC/RC ADPKD mice (while preserving tolvaptan's cyst-attenuating efficacy) and reduced urine volume and nocturia frequency in a Phase 2 trial of tolvaptan-treated ADPKD patients.

**Why people may care:** Aquaresis (polyuria, nocturia) is the principal tolerability and adherence barrier for tolvaptan, so an add-on that reduces the aquaretic burden without sacrificing disease-modifying efficacy could meaningfully improve real-world use of an otherwise effective therapy.

**What limits confidence:** The "preserved efficacy" claim is demonstrated only in mice — ADPKD has a poor preclinical-to-clinical translation record — and the supporting Phase 2 human data are reported in the abstract without sample size, effect magnitude, or trial-design detail.

**Classification:** pipeline-relevant
**Study design:** preclinical (cell + Pkd1RC/RC mouse) plus an interventional Phase 2 human component
**Publication event:** full-paper-published
**Endpoint:** surrogate — tolvaptan-induced urine volume/polyuria and nocturia (tolerability), plus preclinical cyst-burden attenuation
**Comparator:** placebo (probenecid vs control; add-on to tolvaptan)
**Result:** positive
**Skepticism flags:** Surrogate endpoints only; efficacy-preservation shown in mice only; Phase 2 human data lack reported n/effect size in abstract
