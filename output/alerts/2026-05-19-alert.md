## ALERT — Practice-Relevant — Pregnancy not associated with ADPKD progression in HALT-PKD secondary analysis

**What happened:** A secondary analysis of the HALT-PKD trials (n=455 women, median age 45, eGFR 69 ml/min/1.73 m²) found no association between pregnancy history (0 vs 1-2 vs ≥3 prior pregnancies) and annual eGFR slope, %ΔTKV, or composite kidney failure in multivariable adjusted models; a propensity-matched comparison of women who became pregnant during study participation (n=13) vs non-pregnant matched controls also showed no difference in eGFR slope (Beta: -0.14 [95% CI: -2.72, 2.44]) or TKV change (Beta: 0.04 [-3.74, 3.82]).

**Why people may care:** Whether pregnancy accelerates ADPKD progression has been clinically uncertain for decades; this null result from the best-characterised ADPKD cohort (HALT-PKD) offers the most rigorous published data yet and is directly relevant to counselling reproductive-age women with ADPKD.

**What limits confidence:** The in-study pregnancy comparison is n=13 (very wide confidence intervals; absence of detected effect ≠ absence of effect), median participant age of 45 years means this cohort does not represent women who are most likely to become pregnant, follow-up was 2.5–4 years (short for a slowly progressive disease), and pregnancy was self-reported rather than registry-verified.

**Classification:** practice-relevant

**Study design:** Observational-retrospective secondary analysis of prospective RCT cohort (HALT-PKD Studies A and B)
**Publication event:** full-paper-published (JASN)
**Endpoint:** Surrogate (eGFR slope, %ΔTKV) and patient-important (composite: kidney failure, 50% eGFR decline, death)
**Comparator:** Propensity-matched non-pregnant women (1:4); categorical comparison (no pregnancy vs 1-2 vs ≥3 prior pregnancies)
**Result:** Negative — no association detected across all analyses
**Skepticism flags:** Retrospective, propensity matched, small n (n=13 in key comparison), short follow-up for slow disease, surrogate endpoints primary, self-reported pregnancy

### Pediatric cardiac remodelling in ADPKD — Heming et al, Pediatric Nephrology

Single-centre cross-sectional study (n=116 children, <18 years). Hypertension in 12% (n=14), including 8 with masked hypertension. Children with High BP (≥90th percentile, n=30) had higher cyst burden (56% vs 34%, p=0.036) and LV hypertrophy (16.6% vs 4.6%, p=0.034) compared to normotensives.

**What limits confidence:** Single-centre, cross-sectional; small High BP subgroup (n=30); cross-sectional design cannot establish causation or progression trajectory.
