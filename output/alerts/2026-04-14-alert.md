# ADPKD Research Alert — 2026-04-14

## ALERT — Pipeline Milestone — Farabursen (miR-17 inhibitor) reports first clinical efficacy data: polycystin restoration and htTKV growth reduction in ADPKD patients

**What happened:** A WCN26 conference abstract (WCN26-7987) reported that farabursen (formerly RGLS8429), a miR-17 inhibitor developed by Regulus Therapeutics, increases urinary polycystin-1 and polycystin-2 and reduces height-adjusted total kidney volume (htTKV) growth in ADPKD patients, based on data from the completed Phase 1 trial (NCT05521191).

**Why people may care:** This is the first novel-mechanism drug to demonstrate both pharmacodynamic proof of concept (polycystin restoration in human patients) and a structural benefit (TKV growth reduction), validating the miR-17 inhibition approach as a potential ADPKD therapy distinct from vasopressin receptor antagonism.

**What limits confidence:** This is a conference abstract title only — no full abstract text, methods, sample size, statistical analyses, or effect magnitudes are available; the trial was Phase 1 (primarily designed for safety/PK, not efficacy); likely small sample size; single-arm design with surrogate endpoints only; company-sponsored study. The polycystin-to-TKV translation pathway is plausible but the magnitude of clinical benefit is entirely unknown from this disclosure.

**Classification:** pipeline-relevant

**Study design:** Phase 1, interventional, likely single-arm (NCT05521191)
**Publication event:** abstract-available (WCN26 conference abstract in Kidney International Reports)
**Endpoint:** surrogate — htTKV growth rate, urinary polycystin-1 and polycystin-2 (pharmacodynamic biomarkers)
**Comparator:** baseline-only (Phase 1 design)
**Result:** positive
**Skepticism flags:** Abstract-only disclosure; no full methods available; company-funded study of company product; surrogate endpoint only; small n (Phase 1); single-arm design

## Additional notable items this week (below alert threshold)

### WCN26-7921: 12-month ketogenic metabolic intervention in Japanese ADPKD patients

**What happened:** Weimbs, Horie, and colleagues presented 12-month interim results from a ketogenic metabolic intervention study in Japanese ADPKD patients at WCN26.

**Why people may care:** Extends the KETO-ADPKD line of evidence to a non-European population with longer follow-up (12 months vs the original 3 months), addressing two key limitations of prior data.

**What limits confidence:** Conference abstract only; likely single-arm with pre/post comparison; Weimbs has commercial ties to Santa Barbara Nutrients (Ren-Nu); no full data, methods, or sample size available.

### WCN26-4862: TKV changes with dapagliflozin in ADPKD

**What happened:** ElSharkawy et al. presented data on total kidney volume changes in ADPKD patients treated with dapagliflozin at WCN26.

**Why people may care:** Addresses whether SGLT2 inhibitor benefits in ADPKD extend to the structural endpoint (TKV), not just functional measures (eGFR) — a key open question as STOP-PKD (NCT07280585) and EMPA-PKD (NCT06391450) continue recruiting.

**What limits confidence:** Conference abstract only; likely small sample, single-arm; definitive data will come from the ongoing Phase 3 STOP-PKD trial.

### ALDH1A1 as novel ADPKD target (Kidney International)

**What happened:** A KI commentary (Zhang & He) highlighted ALDH1A1 as a novel therapeutic target in ADPKD, based on mouse data showing disulfiram (an approved drug) and anti-PD-L1 combination therapy reduces cyst progression.

**Why people may care:** Drug repurposing angle (disulfiram is FDA-approved for alcohol dependence) combined with a novel immune checkpoint approach to ADPKD.

**What limits confidence:** Entirely preclinical mouse data; ADPKD has historically poor preclinical-to-clinical translation; immunotherapy for a genetic kidney disease is speculative.
