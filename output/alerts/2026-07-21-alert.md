## ALERT — Practice-relevant — Three-year randomized trial of lanreotide did not slow measured GFR decline in stage 2/3 ADPKD

**What happened:** In the LIPS trial (NCT02127437), 144 adults with ADPKD and
measured GFR 30–89 mL/min/1.73 m² were randomized to monthly lanreotide 120 mg or
placebo for three years; the primary endpoint of iohexol-measured GFR slope was not
met, with an annualized between-arm difference of −0.3 mL/min/1.73 m²/year
(95% CI −3.4 to 2.8).

**Why people may care:** This is the most methodologically rigorous randomized test
of a somatostatin analogue in ADPKD to date — it used measured rather than estimated
GFR over a three-year horizon — and it does not support lanreotide for kidney-function
protection in this population; a previously under-emphasized hypoglycemia signal
(11.1% vs 1.4%) also emerged.

**What limits confidence:** Recruitment stopped early at 144 of 180 planned
participants, so the confidence interval (−3.4 to +2.8 mL/min/1.73 m²/year) cannot
exclude a modest benefit or harm; the trial completed data collection in July 2019 and
is only now published; and the results apply only to adults with mGFR 30–89
mL/min/1.73 m².

**Classification:** practice-relevant

**Study design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 3 years, intention-to-treat, iohexol-clearance primary endpoint
**Publication event:** full-paper-published (online ahead of print, 2026-06-19) — underlying trial completed 2019-07-31
**Endpoint:** surrogate — measured GFR slope (iohexol clearance); secondary kidney events, quality of life, safety
**Comparator:** placebo (0.9% sodium chloride injection)
**Result:** negative (primary endpoint not met)
**Skepticism flags:** Surrogate endpoint only (measured GFR rather than kidney failure); underpowered after early recruitment stop; 7-year lag between trial completion and publication

**Source:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42320793/ — Joly D, et al. *Kidney International*, 2026 Jun 19. doi:10.1016/j.kint.2026.05.018

### Note on the secondary creatinine signal

The trial reported a statistically significant creatinine-based eGFR advantage for
lanreotide (+1.2 mL/min/1.73 m²/year, 95% CI 0.27–2.15). The authors explicitly state
this was **not** reproduced by cystatin C-eGFR or urinary creatinine clearance, and
attribute it to a non-GFR effect on creatinine physiology. This secondary result should
not be read as evidence of benefit, and it is a textbook illustration of why
creatinine-based eGFR is a fragile endpoint in ADPKD trials.

This point is reinforced by an independent preprint published in the same window
(`ppr_1279948`, Groothof/Bakker, Open Research Europe, 2026-07-16), which argues that
treatment-related changes in creatinine generation may bias tolvaptan efficacy
estimates in TEMPO 3:4 and calls for a cystatin C reanalysis. Two independent lines of
argument converging on the same methodological concern is worth carrying into the next
deep analysis — though neither constitutes evidence that tolvaptan's benefit is
overstated.

## ALERT — Pipeline-relevant — First results posted for CAM2029 (octreotide subcutaneous depot) in polycystic liver disease: liver-volume benefit at the edge of significance

**What happened:** Camurus posted registry results on 2026-06-01 for NCT05281328, a
Phase 2/3 placebo-controlled trial of octreotide subcutaneous depot (CAM2029) in 71
patients with polycystic liver disease; the primary endpoint of height-adjusted total
liver volume change at Week 53 gave an estimated relative treatment difference of
−4.3% (95% CI −8.4 to −0.1, p=0.0443, ANCOVA).

**Why people may care:** Polycystic liver disease is a common and symptomatic
extrarenal manifestation of ADPKD with few options, and this is the first randomized
readout for a new somatostatin-analogue formulation designed for subcutaneous depot
delivery.

**What limits confidence:** The result sits at the edge of statistical significance
(p=0.0443, CI upper bound −0.1%) and is driven as much by liver growth in the small
placebo arm (+3.9%, n=21) as by shrinkage on treatment (−0.7%); the endpoint is
volumetric rather than symptomatic, all 13 secondary endpoints including htTKV, eGFR
and the PLD-Q/SF-36 quality-of-life measures remain unpopulated, one death occurred in
the Q2W arm, and these are registry-posted results that have not been peer-reviewed.

**Classification:** pipeline-relevant

**Study design:** Phase 2/3 randomized placebo-controlled trial, n=71 (24 QW / 23 Q2W / 23 placebo started), Week 53 primary analysis
**Publication event:** results-posted (registry results first posted 2026-06-01; submitted 2026-03-05) — not peer-reviewed
**Endpoint:** surrogate — height-adjusted total liver volume at Week 53
**Comparator:** placebo
**Result:** positive (marginally; primary endpoint met at p=0.0443)
**Skepticism flags:** Company-sponsored study of company product; surrogate volumetric endpoint only; small placebo arm (n=21); bare-threshold p-value; secondary and quality-of-life endpoints not yet reported; results-posted without peer review; one death in a treatment arm

## ALERT — Pipeline-relevant — GSK4771261 ADPKD Phase 1 closes enrolment and slips readout by ~7 months

**What happened:** NCT06734234, GlaxoSmithKline's single-ascending-dose Phase 1 study of
GSK4771261 in healthy participants and ADPKD patients, changed status from RECRUITING to
ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING on 2026-07-15, with enrolment revised from 84 to 86 (estimated) and
estimated primary completion moved from 2026-09-04 to 2027-04-23.

**Why people may care:** This is one of relatively few novel-mechanism ADPKD assets to
have entered human testing, and enrolment closing means dosing is complete or nearly so —
but the readout that had been expected this year is now a 2027 event.

**What limits confidence:** ClinicalTrials.gov gives no reason for the roughly
seven-month extension, so the change is equally consistent with routine follow-up
lengthening, protocol amendment, or a problem; no results are posted and no efficacy or

- **`pm_42445794` — "Halting cyst progression in ADPKD using long-term ketogenic
  metabolic therapy…" (*Frontiers in Nutrition*).** Scored down aggressively
  (importance 3, evidence 1, claim calibration 3). This is an uncontrolled retrospective
  case series of **four self-referred patients**, each serving as their own historical
  comparator, using a commercial medical food (KetoCitra®). Two authors were employees
  of the manufacturer; the senior author is a shareholder, holds a managerial position,
  and is a named inventor on related patents. The title's claim is not supported by the
  design.

  Directly counterpointed by `pm_42439786` (*Nutrition in Clinical Practice*), a single
  case on a plant-based ketogenic diet in which albuminuria improved from 80 to 10 mg/g
  **while total kidney volume rose from 617 to 709 cc** — roughly 15% growth. Biomarker
  improvement and cyst-burden progression moved in opposite directions in the same patient.

- **`pm_42418222` — Smooth muscle cell polycystin-1 is dispensable for physiological

  result contradicting inferences from older germline knockout models. Recorded here
  per the negative-result policy rather than omitted for being unexciting; it weakens
  the smooth-muscle mechanism for ADPKD hypertension.
