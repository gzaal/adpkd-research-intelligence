# ADPKD Patient Community & Conferences

> **Last updated:** April 2026
>
> This document is a living synthesis of the ADPKD patient community landscape: conferences where new research is presented, organizations that support patients, registries collecting real-world data, quality-of-life research, and the institutions driving ADPKD science forward. It is written for patients and families, with technical terms explained where they appear.

---

## 1. Major Conferences

Scientific conferences are where researchers first present new findings -- often months before formal journal publication. For patients, conference abstracts and press releases are the earliest window into what is coming next.

### PKDCON

PKDCON is the dedicated PKD conference, organized with support from the PKD Foundation. It brings together basic scientists, nephrologists, and patient advocates to focus exclusively on polycystic kidney disease. Unlike general nephrology meetings where PKD is one topic among many, PKDCON devotes its entire program to ADPKD and ARPKD (the recessive form). Sessions cover drug development, genetics, dietary interventions, and patient-reported outcomes. The meeting alternates between US and international locations.

### ASN Kidney Week

The American Society of Nephrology (ASN) Kidney Week is the largest nephrology conference in the world, held every autumn in the United States. Each year it includes multiple ADPKD-focused sessions, late-breaking clinical trial results, and poster presentations. Major ADPKD trial readouts -- including data on tolvaptan, VX-407, and dietary interventions -- are frequently debuted at ASN Kidney Week. For patients tracking the research pipeline, the ASN abstract supplement (published in the *Journal of the American Society of Nephrology*) is a key resource each November.

### ERA Congress

The European Renal Association (ERA) Congress, held annually in Europe (typically May or June), is the premier European nephrology meeting. It features many ADPKD presentations, and abstracts are published as supplements to *Nephrology Dialysis Transplantation* (NDT). European-led trials such as KETO-ADPKD (University of Cologne), HYDROPROTECT (UMCG, Groningen), and the leuprorelin trial (also UMCG) often present data here first. The ERA also publishes ADPKD position statements and guideline updates.

### World Congress of Nephrology (WCN)

The World Congress of Nephrology (WCN) is the global meeting of the International Society of Nephrology (ISN), held every two to three years in rotating locations worldwide. The WCN 2026 meeting featured notable ADPKD content, including Phase 1 data for PYC-003, a novel investigational compound. WCN abstracts provide a snapshot of the global ADPKD research effort, including work from regions underrepresented at US or European meetings.

### Other Relevant Meetings

- **ARPKD/ADPKD International Conference** -- periodic meetings focused on pediatric and early-onset disease.
- **National Kidney Foundation (NKF) Spring Clinical Meetings** -- US-based, with some ADPKD content.
- **ISN Frontiers Meetings** -- regional meetings organized by the International Society of Nephrology.

## 2. Patient Foundations & Organizations

### PKD Foundation (United States)

The PKD Foundation is the largest patient advocacy organization dedicated to polycystic kidney disease. Founded in 1982, it funds research, provides patient education, and advocates for regulatory policies that benefit the PKD community. Key activities include:

- **Research funding:** The Foundation awards grants to investigators studying ADPKD and ARPKD, with a focus on translational work (moving discoveries from the lab toward patients).
- **ADPKD Patient Registry:** The Foundation operates the ADPKD Patient Registry (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT04039061), a longitudinal study collecting data from approximately 3,000 patients across the US. The Registry tracks disease progression, treatments, quality of life, and outcomes. It provides researchers with real-world evidence that complements data from clinical trials. As of April 2026, the Registry is ongoing and actively enrolling.
- **Patient education:** The Foundation's website, webinars, and annual Walk for PKD events are the primary touchpoints for many newly diagnosed patients.
- **Advocacy:** The Foundation lobbies for research funding (including NIH appropriations) and has supported expanded access to tolvaptan and other treatments.

**Website:** pkdcure.org

### PKD International

PKD International is the global umbrella organization connecting national PKD patient groups. It coordinates World PKD Day (held annually in September) and facilitates information sharing across countries. For patients outside the United States, PKD International can help identify local support groups and resources.

### National Kidney Foundations and PKD Groups

Many countries have national kidney foundations or dedicated PKD organizations:

- **Kidney Research UK** and the **PKD Charity (UK)** -- support research and provide patient resources in the United Kingdom.
- **Nierstichting (Dutch Kidney Foundation)** -- funds kidney research in the Netherlands, including ADPKD projects.
- **PKD Australia** -- patient support and advocacy.
- **AIRP (Associazione Italiana Rene Policistico)** -- Italian PKD association.
- **Deutsche PKD-Gesellschaft** -- German PKD patient organization.
- **Kidney Foundation of Canada** -- funds research across all kidney diseases including ADPKD.

These organizations vary in size, but they share a common mission: connecting patients with information, supporting research, and reducing the isolation that can accompany a rare disease diagnosis.

## 3. Patient Registries

Patient registries collect real-world data from large groups of people living with ADPKD. Unlike clinical trials (which study specific treatments under controlled conditions), registries follow patients over time to understand how the disease progresses, what treatments people receive, and what outcomes they experience. Registry data helps researchers design better trials and helps patients and doctors understand what to expect.

### PKD Foundation ADPKD Registry

- **ClinicalTrials.gov ID:** NCT04039061
- **Enrollment:** approximately 3,000 participants
- **Status:** Ongoing (as of April 2026)
- **Sponsor:** PKD Foundation
- **What it collects:** Patient-reported disease history, treatments, kidney function, quality of life, family history, and genetic testing results.
- **Why it matters:** This is the largest patient-driven ADPKD registry in the US. Because it includes patients who may not be eligible for clinical trials (e.g., those with advanced disease or comorbidities), it reflects the full spectrum of ADPKD more accurately than trial data alone.

### German ADPKD Tolvaptan Treatment Registry

- **ClinicalTrials.gov ID:** NCT02497521
- **Enrollment:** approximately 2,000 participants
- **Sponsor:** University of Cologne
- **What it collects:** Real-world data on tolvaptan use in German ADPKD patients, including efficacy, side effects, liver safety monitoring, and treatment discontinuation rates.
- **Why it matters:** Tolvaptan is the only approved drug specifically for ADPKD, but its real-world use differs from clinical trial conditions. This registry provides evidence on how tolvaptan performs outside the controlled setting of the TEMPO and REPRISE trials, including in patients with comorbidities and varying levels of kidney function.

### Rogosin Institute PKD Data Repository

- **ClinicalTrials.gov ID:** NCT00792155
- **Enrollment:** approximately 1,000 participants
- **Sponsor:** Rogosin Institute (New York)
- **What it collects:** Long-term observational data on ADPKD disease course, including imaging data, kidney function over time, and clinical events.
- **Why it matters:** One of the longest-running PKD observational cohorts, providing data on the natural history of ADPKD before the tolvaptan era.

### UK National Registry of Rare Kidney Diseases (RaDaR)

- **ClinicalTrials.gov ID:** NCT06065852
- **Enrollment:** approximately 35,000 participants (across all rare kidney diseases, including a substantial ADPKD subgroup)
- **Sponsor:** UK-based consortium
- **What it collects:** Clinical data on rare kidney diseases, including ADPKD, linked to UK health records. Tracks long-term outcomes, treatment patterns, and progression to kidney failure.
- **Why it matters:** The large size and linkage to national health data make RaDaR one of the most powerful resources for studying ADPKD outcomes at a population level. It captures patients across the full disease spectrum, including those managed conservatively without tolvaptan.

### Bologna ADPKD Analysis

- **ClinicalTrials.gov ID:** NCT06759142
- **Enrollment:** approximately 180 participants
- **Sponsor:** Bologna, Italy
- **What it collects:** Detailed clinical and imaging data from an Italian ADPKD cohort.
- **Why it matters:** Contributes to the European evidence base and may capture treatment patterns specific to southern European practice.

### Early PKD Observational Cohort

- **ClinicalTrials.gov ID:** NCT02936791
- **Enrollment:** approximately 250 participants
- **Sponsor:** University of Kansas Medical Center
- **What it collects:** Data on early-stage ADPKD (normal or mildly reduced kidney function), including imaging biomarkers, genetic testing, and early progression markers.
- **Why it matters:** Most ADPKD studies focus on patients with established disease. This cohort specifically targets people in the earliest stages, when interventions might have the greatest impact. It helps define what "early" ADPKD looks like and which markers predict future progression.

## 4. Quality of Life

ADPKD affects more than kidney function. Patients often experience chronic pain, fatigue, anxiety about disease progression, lifestyle restrictions (especially with tolvaptan's requirement for high water intake and frequent urination), and the psychological burden of a hereditary condition. Quality-of-life (QoL) research aims to measure and address these impacts.

### Patient-Reported Outcome (PRO) Tools

Patient-reported outcome measures are standardized questionnaires that capture how patients feel and function. In ADPKD, commonly used PRO tools include:

- **ADPKD-specific instruments** that measure kidney-related symptoms (flank pain, abdominal fullness, early satiety) and their impact on daily life.
- **Generic QoL measures** such as the SF-36 (Short Form 36) and EQ-5D, which allow comparison across diseases.
- **ADPKD Pain and Discomfort Scale** -- instruments designed to capture the specific pain experience of ADPKD, which differs from typical chronic pain.

Regulatory agencies (FDA, EMA) increasingly expect clinical trials to include PRO endpoints, meaning that new drugs must demonstrate not only that they slow disease progression but that patients feel better or at least no worse.

### Somatosensory Phenotyping of ADPKD Pain (KU Leuven)

- **ClinicalTrials.gov ID:** NCT06969028
- **Sponsor:** KU Leuven (Belgium)
- **What it studies:** This study uses advanced pain testing (somatosensory phenotyping) to characterize the mechanisms underlying pain in ADPKD. Rather than simply asking "how much pain do you have," it measures how the nervous system processes pain signals in ADPKD patients.
- **Why it matters:** ADPKD pain is often undertreated because its mechanisms are poorly understood. Some patients have cyst-related pain (mechanical pressure), while others may develop central sensitization (where the nervous system amplifies pain signals). Understanding the type of pain can guide treatment -- anti-inflammatory drugs, nerve blocks, or centrally-acting agents may be needed for different pain subtypes.

### Shared Decision-Making Model (Seoul National University)

- **ClinicalTrials.gov ID:** NCT06618638
- **Sponsor:** Seoul National University (South Korea)
- **What it studies:** Developing and evaluating a shared decision-making model for ADPKD treatment decisions, particularly around tolvaptan initiation.
- **Why it matters:** Starting tolvaptan is a significant decision: the drug slows disease progression but comes with substantial side effects (polyuria, thirst, liver monitoring). Patients weigh the long-term benefit of slower progression against the daily burden of side effects. A structured shared decision-making approach helps patients and doctors discuss trade-offs openly, leading to decisions that align with individual values and priorities.

### HYDROPROTECT and QoL Endpoints

The HYDROPROTECT trial (UMCG, Groningen; testing water prescription as an alternative to tolvaptan) includes quality-of-life endpoints. This is notable because one of the main motivations for the water-only approach is that it might achieve similar vasopressin suppression (the key mechanism) with a lower side-effect burden than tolvaptan. QoL data from HYDROPROTECT will directly address whether patients tolerate prescribed water intake better than tolvaptan.

### Tolvaptan Side-Effect Burden

Tolvaptan remains the only approved ADPKD-specific therapy (as of April 2026), but its side effects meaningfully affect quality of life:

- **Polyuria and polydipsia:** Patients typically urinate 6-8 liters per day and must drink large amounts of water to compensate, including overnight. This disrupts sleep, limits travel and work flexibility, and requires constant access to restrooms.
- **Liver safety monitoring:** Regular blood tests for liver function are required, and a small proportion of patients develop liver enzyme elevations requiring dose reduction or discontinuation.
- **Discontinuation rates:** Real-world registry data (including from the German Tolvaptan Registry, NCT02497521) show that a meaningful proportion of patients discontinue tolvaptan due to intolerable side effects, even when the drug is effectively slowing disease.

These burdens underscore why the ADPKD community is eager for alternative treatments (VX-407, dietary interventions, ABBV-CLS-628, and others in the pipeline) that might offer disease-modifying benefit with a more manageable side-effect profile.

## 5. Active Research Groups

The ADPKD research landscape in 2026 is more active than at any previous point. Below are the institutions and organizations running multiple ADPKD studies, grouped by their primary focus. Patients enrolled in -- or considering -- clinical trials may encounter these groups.

### Academic Medical Centers

**Mayo Clinic (Rochester, Minnesota, USA)**
Mayo is one of the most prolific ADPKD research centers globally. Their contributions include the Mayo Clinic Imaging Classification for ADPKD (a widely used tool for predicting disease progression based on kidney size and shape), multiple observational studies tracking ADPKD natural history, and ongoing work on imaging biomarkers including AI-assisted kidney volume measurement. Mayo data underpins many of the risk-stratification tools used in clinical practice today.

**University of Cologne (Germany)**
Cologne is a leading European ADPKD center with multiple active studies:
- **KETO-ADPKD** (NCT04680780) -- completed trial of ketogenic diet in ADPKD, with results suggesting kidney volume reduction when sufficient ketosis was achieved.
- **German ADPKD Tolvaptan Treatment Registry** (NCT02497521) -- the major European real-world tolvaptan data source.
- **STOP-PKD** -- additional interventional work.

The Cologne group, led by Prof. Roman-Ulrich Muller and colleagues, has been central to establishing the evidence base for dietary interventions in ADPKD.

**University Medical Center Groningen / UMCG (Netherlands)**
UMCG is a hub for ADPKD research in the Netherlands and a leader in innovative trial design:
- **HYDROPROTECT** -- testing prescribed high water intake as a non-drug alternative to vasopressin suppression, with QoL endpoints.
- **Leuprorelin trial** -- investigating a GnRH agonist for its effect on cyst growth, based on the observation that hormonal pathways influence cyst proliferation.

UMCG's work is particularly relevant for patients in the Netherlands and for those interested in non-pharmacological approaches.

**University of Queensland (Australia)**
- **IMPEDE-PKD** -- a trial investigating metformin in combination with other agents to slow ADPKD progression. The University of Queensland group brings strong clinical trial infrastructure in the Asia-Pacific region.

**University of Colorado (USA)**
- Running a GLP-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA) trial in ADPKD. GLP-1 RAs are widely used in type 2 diabetes and obesity (drugs like semaglutide/Ozempic). Preclinical data suggest they may slow cyst growth through mechanisms independent of their metabolic effects. This is an example of drug repurposing -- testing an established drug in a new disease. The Colorado trial will provide the first human ADPKD-specific data for this drug class.

**Children's Hospital of Philadelphia (CHOP, USA)**
CHOP operates an ARPKD registry and conducts research on pediatric kidney disease, including ADPKD studies. Early-onset ADPKD in children is an area where natural history data is especially needed, as most treatment evidence comes from adult populations.

**Mario Negri Institute (Bergamo, Italy)**
A renowned nephrology research institute contributing work on RNA-seq diagnostics -- using gene expression profiling (RNA sequencing) to improve ADPKD diagnosis and possibly identify molecular subtypes of the disease that respond differently to treatment.

**KU Leuven (Belgium)**
Running the somatosensory phenotyping study on ADPKD pain (NCT06969028, described above).

**Seoul National University (South Korea)**
Investigating shared decision-making models for ADPKD treatment (NCT06618638, described above).

**University of Kansas Medical Center (USA)**
Operating the Early PKD Observational Cohort (NCT02936791), studying ADPKD in its earliest stages.

### Pharmaceutical & Biotech Companies

**Otsuka Pharmaceutical**
Otsuka developed and markets tolvaptan (Jynarque/Samsca), the only approved ADPKD therapy. Otsuka continues to run long-term extension studies and supports real-world evidence generation. Any new ADPKD drug will effectively be compared against tolvaptan, making Otsuka's ongoing data collection a benchmark for the field.

**Vertex Pharmaceuticals**
Vertex is developing VX-407, a PKD1 corrector, in the Phase 2 AGLOW trial. The concept behind VX-407 is analogous to what Vertex achieved in cystic fibrosis: restoring function to a defective protein (in this case, polycystin-1, the protein encoded by the PKD1 gene). If VX-407 works, it would be the first treatment to address the root cause of ADPKD rather than treating downstream effects. The AGLOW trial is one of the most closely watched studies in the ADPKD pipeline as of April 2026.

**AbbVie**
AbbVie is developing ABBV-CLS-628, a novel compound for ADPKD. Details on the mechanism have been emerging through early-phase clinical data. AbbVie's entry into the ADPKD space signals growing pharmaceutical industry interest in the disease, which has historically been underserved relative to its prevalence (affecting approximately 1 in 1,000 people worldwide).

## 6. Patient Perspective: What This Means for You

### If You Are Newly Diagnosed

Being diagnosed with ADPKD can feel overwhelming, but the research landscape in 2026 offers more reason for cautious optimism than at any previous time:

- **One approved treatment exists today.** Tolvaptan (Jynarque) slows kidney growth and delays progression to kidney failure. It is not a cure, and it comes with significant side effects, but for patients with rapidly progressing disease, it is a meaningful option. Not all patients need or benefit from tolvaptan -- your nephrologist can help determine your risk level using tools like the Mayo Classification and htTKV (height-adjusted total kidney volume).

- **Multiple new drugs are in clinical trials.** VX-407 (Vertex) aims to fix the underlying protein defect. ABBV-CLS-628 (AbbVie) represents another novel approach. GLP-1 receptor agonists and SGLT2 inhibitors -- drugs already used for diabetes -- are being tested specifically in ADPKD. The pipeline is broader than it has ever been.

- **Dietary approaches are gaining evidence.** The KETO-ADPKD trial showed that ketogenic diets may slow kidney growth. Trials of caloric restriction, modified Atkins diets, and prescribed water intake are ongoing. While dietary interventions are not a substitute for medical treatment in rapidly progressing disease, they represent an accessible strategy that patients can discuss with their care teams.

- **You are not alone.** The PKD Foundation (US), PKD International, and national kidney organizations connect thousands of patients. Patient registries like the PKD Foundation Registry (NCT04039061) allow you to contribute to research even if you are not in a clinical trial.

### If You Are Monitoring Your Condition

For patients who have been living with ADPKD and are tracking their disease:

- **Ask about your risk classification.** If you have had an MRI, ask whether your doctor has assessed your Mayo Classification or htTKV. These tools help predict how quickly your disease may progress and whether treatment is warranted.

- **Stay informed about trials.** ClinicalTrials.gov (search "ADPKD") lists all registered studies with eligibility criteria. Your nephrologist or the PKD Foundation can help identify trials you might be eligible for.

- **Quality of life matters.** If you are on tolvaptan and struggling with side effects, know that research is actively working on this problem. The HYDROPROTECT trial and shared decision-making studies recognize that treatment burden is a real issue, not just a side effect to tolerate.

- **Pain is a legitimate concern.** ADPKD pain is increasingly recognized as a distinct clinical problem. The KU Leuven somatosensory phenotyping study (NCT06969028) aims to improve understanding and treatment of ADPKD pain. If your pain is not well managed, seek care from a nephrologist experienced with ADPKD or a pain specialist familiar with the condition.

### The Big Picture (April 2026)

The ADPKD research field is experiencing a period of unprecedented activity:

- **Drug development:** More candidate drugs are in clinical trials now than at any point in history. The pipeline spans multiple mechanisms of action, from protein correctors (VX-407) to metabolic modulators (GLP-1 RAs, ketone-based therapies) to novel compounds (ABBV-CLS-628).

- **Dietary and lifestyle science:** What was once anecdotal advice is becoming evidence-based, with completed and ongoing randomized trials of ketogenic diets, caloric restriction, and water intake.

- **Precision medicine:** Genetic testing and biomarker research are moving toward matching patients with the treatments most likely to benefit them, rather than a one-size-fits-all approach.

- **Patient voice:** Registries, PRO tools, and shared decision-making research reflect a field that increasingly values the patient experience alongside laboratory and imaging endpoints.

For a disease that had no approved treatment as recently as 2015 (when tolvaptan was approved in the US for ADPKD), this level of activity is a meaningful shift. Results from many of the studies described here are expected over the next one to three years.
