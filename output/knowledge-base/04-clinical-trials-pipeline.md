## How to Read This Document

Clinical trials progress through phases:

- **Phase 1** -- Tests safety and dosing in a small number of people. The question is: "Is this safe?"
- **Phase 2** -- Tests whether the drug works and refines dosing in a larger group. The question is: "Does this show signs of benefit?"
- **Phase 3** -- Large-scale trials that compare the new treatment against placebo or standard care. The question is: "Does this reliably work, and do benefits outweigh risks?"

Larger enrollment numbers (n) generally mean stronger evidence. Primary completion dates indicate when the main results should be available; study completion dates include longer follow-up.

## Currently Recruiting Interventional Trials (as of April 2026)

### Phase 3 Trials

These are the largest and most advanced trials. Positive Phase 3 results typically lead to regulatory approval.

#### IMPEDE-PKD -- Metformin Extended Release
- **NCT ID:** [NCT04939935](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04939935)
- **Intervention:** Metformin XR (extended-release), a widely used diabetes drug being repurposed for ADPKD
- **Enrollment:** 1,174 participants
- **Sponsor:** University of Queensland (Australia)
- **Phase:** 3
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in total kidney volume (TKV) measured by MRI
- **Why it matters:** Metformin targets metabolic reprogramming in cyst cells. It is inexpensive, well-understood, and already approved for other uses. The large enrollment makes this one of the most statistically powerful ADPKD trials ever conducted. Builds on earlier TAME-PKD Phase 2 data showing a signal of benefit.
- **Expected primary completion:** ~2027 (verify on ClinicalTrials.gov for latest estimate)
- **Key consideration:** If positive, metformin could become a cheap, widely accessible add-on therapy alongside tolvaptan.

#### STOP-PKD -- Dapagliflozin (SGLT2 Inhibitor)
- **NCT ID:** [NCT07280585](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07280585)
- **Intervention:** Dapagliflozin (Forxiga), an SGLT2 inhibitor originally developed for diabetes and heart failure
- **Enrollment:** 420 participants
- **Sponsor:** University of Cologne (Germany)
- **Phase:** 3
- **Primary endpoint:** Change in TKV
- **Why it matters:** SGLT2 inhibitors have shown kidney-protective effects in diabetic and non-diabetic chronic kidney disease (the landmark DAPA-CKD and EMPA-KIDNEY trials). This trial tests whether those benefits extend specifically to ADPKD, which has a distinct pathophysiology driven by cyst growth rather than just filtration decline. European consortium-led trial.
- **Expected primary completion:** ~2028 (verify on ClinicalTrials.gov)
- **Key consideration:** SGLT2 inhibitors are already widely prescribed for CKD, so some ADPKD patients may already be taking them. This trial will clarify whether there is a specific benefit for cyst growth.

#### HYDROPROTECT -- Hydrochlorothiazide + Tolvaptan Combination
- **NCT ID:** [NCT05373264](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05373264)
- **Intervention:** Hydrochlorothiazide (a common diuretic) added to tolvaptan
- **Enrollment:** 300 participants
- **Sponsor:** University Medical Center Groningen, UMCG (Netherlands)
- **Phase:** 3
- **Primary endpoint:** Improvement in tolvaptan tolerability (aquaretic side effects) while maintaining efficacy
- **Why it matters:** Tolvaptan (Jinarc/Jynarque) is the only approved disease-modifying drug for ADPKD, but many patients struggle with its side effects -- excessive thirst and frequent urination caused by its mechanism of blocking vasopressin. This Dutch trial tests whether adding a low-dose thiazide diuretic can reduce those aquaretic effects, making tolvaptan more tolerable and improving adherence.
- **Expected primary completion:** ~2026-2027 (verify on ClinicalTrials.gov)
- **Key consideration:** A practical trial. If successful, it would not introduce a new drug but rather make the existing approved therapy more livable for the many patients who discontinue or reduce tolvaptan due to side effects.

#### Pediatric Tolvaptan Suspension
- **NCT ID:** [NCT04782258](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04782258)
- **Intervention:** Tolvaptan oral suspension formulated for infants and young children
- **Enrollment:** ~20 participants
- **Sponsor:** Otsuka Pharmaceutical
- **Phase:** 3
- **Why it matters:** Tolvaptan is currently approved for adults. Children with early-onset, rapidly progressive ADPKD may benefit from earlier intervention, but no child-appropriate formulation existed. This trial evaluates safety, pharmacokinetics, and tolerability of a liquid suspension in a very young population.
- **Expected primary completion:** ~2026 (verify on ClinicalTrials.gov)
- **Key consideration:** Small trial focused on safety in a vulnerable population. Results would potentially open a new treatment window for severely affected children.

### Phase 2 Trials

These proof-of-concept trials test whether newer approaches show enough promise to justify large Phase 3 studies.

#### ABBV-CLS-628 -- AbbVie Novel Agent
- **NCT ID:** [NCT06902558](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06902558)
- **Intervention:** ABBV-CLS-628, a novel compound (mechanism not yet fully disclosed publicly)
- **Enrollment:** 240 participants
- **Sponsor:** AbbVie
- **Phase:** 2
- **Why it matters:** AbbVie is a major pharmaceutical company entering the ADPKD space. The relatively large Phase 2 enrollment (240) suggests confidence in the compound and an intent to generate robust data. Details on the mechanism of action may become clearer as the trial progresses.
- **Expected primary completion:** ~2027-2028 (verify on ClinicalTrials.gov)

#### JMKX003142 -- Jemincare
- **NCT ID:** [NCT06800651](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06800651)
- **Intervention:** JMKX003142, investigational agent
- **Enrollment:** 135 participants
- **Sponsor:** Jemincare (China-based pharmaceutical company)
- **Phase:** 2
- **Why it matters:** Represents growing interest in ADPKD drug development from Asian pharmaceutical companies, potentially expanding the global pipeline.
- **Expected primary completion:** Verify on ClinicalTrials.gov

#### GLP-1 Receptor Agonist / Tirzepatide
- **NCT ID:** [NCT06582875](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06582875)
- **Intervention:** Tirzepatide (Mounjaro/Zepbound), a dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist originally developed for diabetes and obesity
- **Enrollment:** 126 participants
- **Sponsor:** University of Colorado
- **Phase:** 2
- **Primary endpoint:** Likely TKV change and/or metabolic parameters
- **Why it matters:** GLP-1 receptor agonists are among the most transformative drug classes in modern medicine, with proven benefits for diabetes, obesity, cardiovascular disease, and CKD. Preclinical data suggest they may also reduce cyst growth in PKD through metabolic pathways. Tirzepatide is a dual-action agent (GIP + GLP-1), potentially offering enhanced effects. This is an academic-led repurposing trial.
- **Expected primary completion:** ~2027 (verify on ClinicalTrials.gov)
- **Key consideration:** If GLP-1 agents show benefit in ADPKD, it would represent a major new therapeutic avenue using drugs that are already widely available (though expensive and supply-constrained as of 2026).

#### VX-407 AGLOW -- Vertex PKD1 Corrector
- **NCT ID:** [NCT07161037](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07161037)
- **Intervention:** VX-407, a small-molecule PKD1 protein corrector
- **Enrollment:** 26 participants (finalized; see update below)
- **Sponsor:** Vertex Pharmaceuticals
- **Phase:** 2a (early Phase 2, dose-finding)
- **Status update (August 2026):** Enrollment closed on 2026-07-30 at 26 participants (up from an earlier estimate of 24); the trial moved from RECRUITING to **ACTIVE, NOT RECRUITING**. This is a registry status/enrollment change only -- no efficacy, safety, or interim data have been posted. Primary completion remains estimated at 2027-07-22.
- **Why it matters:** This is potentially the most scientifically groundbreaking trial in the current pipeline. VX-407 is designed to directly correct the underlying protein defect caused by certain PKD1 mutations -- conceptually similar to what Vertex achieved with CFTR modulators for cystic fibrosis (e.g., Trikafta). Rather than treating downstream consequences of cyst growth, this drug targets the root cause. The AGLOW trial is a first-in-class approach for ADPKD.
- **Expected primary completion:** 2027-07-22
- **Key considerations:**
  - Only applicable to patients with specific PKD1 missense mutations (a subset of all ADPKD patients -- roughly 25-30% carry missense variants)
  - Small enrollment (26) is typical for early Phase 2a
  - Vertex has deep experience in protein-corrector drug development from its cystic fibrosis program
  - If successful, this would mark a shift toward precision medicine in ADPKD
  - Now that enrollment is closed, the next milestone to watch for is a first safety/efficacy readout

#### Empagliflozin -- Short-Term SGLT2 Inhibitor Effects
- **NCT ID:** [NCT06435858](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06435858)
- **Intervention:** Empagliflozin (Jardiance), another SGLT2 inhibitor
- **Enrollment:** 40 participants
- **Sponsor:** Cantonal Hospital Graubuenden (Switzerland)
- **Phase:** 2
- **Why it matters:** Complements the larger STOP-PKD trial by examining short-term mechanistic effects of SGLT2 inhibition in ADPKD. Smaller mechanistic studies like this help researchers understand *how* a drug works in ADPKD specifically, even before large efficacy trials report.
- **Expected primary completion:** ~2026-2027 (verify on ClinicalTrials.gov)

#### Empagliflozin Feasibility Study -- University of Colorado
- **NCT ID:** [NCT05510115](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05510115)
- **Intervention:** Empagliflozin (Jardiance) vs. placebo
- **Enrollment:** Not disclosed in registry
- **Sponsor:** University of Colorado, Denver
- **Phase:** 2
- **Status:** ACTIVE_NOT_RECRUITING; primary completion now confirmed as **reached** (March 18, 2026, actual, per July 2026 registry update) with **no results posted yet** — now ~4.5 months overdue as of August 2026.
- **Why it matters:** A second, independently run feasibility study of SGLT2 inhibition in ADPKD from a major US academic center. Complementary to the Cantonal Hospital Graubuenden empagliflozin trial (NCT06435858) and the larger STOP-PKD dapagliflozin trial (NCT07280585). Results from this feasibility study could inform design of larger US-based SGLT2i trials.
- **Key consideration:** Small feasibility trial; results will not be definitive, but will provide mechanistic and safety data relevant to the larger STOP-PKD question. This is currently the highest-priority "overdue results" item to watch.

#### Leuprorelin -- GnRH Agonist for Polycystic Liver Disease
- **NCT ID:** [NCT05478083](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05478083)
- **Intervention:** Leuprorelin (a gonadotropin-releasing hormone agonist)
- **Enrollment:** 36 participants
- **Sponsor:** University Medical Center Groningen, UMCG (Netherlands)
- **Phase:** 2
- **Primary target:** Polycystic liver disease (PLD), which frequently co-occurs with ADPKD
- **Why it matters:** Many ADPKD patients are more symptomatic from their enlarged polycystic livers than from their kidneys. Liver cyst growth is influenced by estrogen, and GnRH agonists suppress estrogen production. This trial primarily targets women with severely symptomatic polycystic liver disease. Another Dutch trial from the UMCG group, which is a leading center for PLD research.
- **Expected primary completion:** ~2026-2027 (verify on ClinicalTrials.gov)
- **Key consideration:** Relevant primarily for female ADPKD patients with significant liver involvement.

### Phase 1 Trials

These early-stage trials focus on safety, tolerability, and appropriate dosing. They represent the newest entries to the pipeline.

#### PYC-003 -- PYC Therapeutics
- **NCT ID:** [NCT06714006](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06714006)
- **Intervention:** PYC-003, an antisense oligonucleotide RNA therapy designed to modulate PKD-relevant gene expression
- **Enrollment:** 166 participants (expanded from 116 per June 8, 2026 registry refresh)

- **Phase:** 1

- **Why it matters:** PYC-003 is the first antisense RNA therapeutic specifically entering clinical evaluation for ADPKD. It represents a mechanistic approach entirely distinct from all current and investigational ADPKD drugs. RNA-based therapies have already transformed the treatment of other genetic diseases (e.g., spinal muscular atrophy, hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis). No safety or efficacy data were disclosed in the WCN26 abstract; this is a safety/dose-finding trial.
- **Expected primary completion:** **November 2028** — extended ~2 years from a prior September 2026 target in the June 8, 2026 registry refresh; first data should therefore not be expected before 2028. Reason for the extension is not disclosed in the registry.

### Non-Drug Interventional Trials

These trials test dietary, device, or procedural interventions that are not traditional pharmaceuticals.

#### Ketogenic Diet -- Ohio State University
- **NCT ID:** [NCT06325644](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06325644)
- **Intervention:** Ketogenic dietary intervention
- **Enrollment:** 20 participants
- **Sponsor:** Ohio State University
- **Why it matters:** Builds on the positive KETO-ADPKD results (see "Recently Completed" below) and growing evidence that metabolic reprogramming through dietary ketosis may slow cyst growth. Multiple preclinical studies have shown that cyst-lining cells are metabolically abnormal and dependent on glucose; restricting glucose through ketosis may "starve" cyst growth.
- **Expected primary completion:** Verify on ClinicalTrials.gov

#### Exogenous Ketones -- Ketone Diol
- **NCT ID:** [NCT06867471](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06867471)
- **Intervention:** Exogenous ketone diol supplement (R-1,3-butanediol or similar)
- **Enrollment:** 43 participants
- **Sponsor:** Godstrup Hospital (Denmark)
- **Why it matters:** An alternative approach to achieving ketosis without requiring a strict ketogenic diet. Exogenous ketone supplements may provide the metabolic benefits of ketosis (beta-hydroxybutyrate elevation) while being easier to adhere to long-term. If exogenous ketones prove effective, they could be a practical daily supplement for ADPKD patients.
- **Expected primary completion:** Verify on ClinicalTrials.gov
- **Key consideration:** Adherence to strict ketogenic diets is challenging. If a supplement can replicate the benefit, it would be far more scalable.

#### Renal Denervation -- RDN-ADPKD
- **NCT ID:** [NCT05460169](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05460169)
- **Intervention:** Catheter-based renal denervation (a minimally invasive procedure that disrupts nerve signaling to the kidneys)
- **Enrollment:** 44 participants
- **Sponsor:** University of Erlangen-Nurnberg (Germany)
- **Why it matters:** Renal denervation is an established technique for resistant hypertension. In ADPKD, overactive renal sympathetic nerves may contribute to both hypertension and cyst growth. This trial tests whether disrupting those nerve signals can slow disease progression. A novel non-pharmacological approach.
- **Expected primary completion:** Verify on ClinicalTrials.gov

## Recently Completed Trials with Results

### KETO-ADPKD (NCT04680780) -- Ketogenic Diet
- **Result:** Positive. Published 2023.
- **Design:** Randomized controlled trial comparing a ketogenic diet, water fasting (time-restricted eating), and a control diet in 66 ADPKD patients at the University of Cologne.
- **Key finding:** Participants who achieved sufficient ketosis (measured by blood beta-hydroxybutyrate levels) showed reduced kidney volume growth compared to controls. The benefit appeared linked to the degree of ketosis achieved rather than the specific dietary method.
- **Significance:** First human RCT evidence supporting the metabolic reprogramming hypothesis in ADPKD. Generated substantial interest in follow-up trials (see ketogenic diet and exogenous ketone trials above). Published in *Cell Metabolism*.
- **Limitation:** Small sample size (n=66), short duration (3 months), and the primary analysis was not the pre-specified intention-to-treat comparison but rather a post-hoc analysis based on achieved ketosis levels. Larger, longer trials are needed.

### Pravastatin in Early ADPKD (Gitomer et al., NCT03273413)
- **Result:** Negative. Published in *Kidney International*, 2025 (online); editorial commentary in *Kidney International*, February 2026.
- **Design:** Double-blind RCT, n=150 adults with early ADPKD, 24-month follow-up.
- **Key findings:** HtTKV annual rate: placebo 3.1%/yr vs. pravastatin 4.3%/yr — not statistically significant, with a numerical trend **unfavorable** to pravastatin. No benefit on mGFR change or renal blood flow.
- **Significance:** This is the definitive negative trial for pravastatin in adult early-stage ADPKD. An editorial by V.E. Torres (Feb 2026) concluded that the data "do not support treatment with statins for the only purpose of delaying disease progression." Statins retain their role for established cardiovascular indications in ADPKD patients.

### Farabursen (RGLS8429) Phase 1 (NCT05521191) — completed April 2026
- **Intervention:** Farabursen (miR-17 inhibitor, Regulus Therapeutics), subcutaneous dosing, multiple ascending doses.
- **Status:** Listed as COMPLETED on ClinicalTrials.gov as of April 2026. First efficacy signals disclosed via WCN26 conference abstract (WCN26-7987, *Kidney International Reports*, April 2026).
- **Key disclosed findings (abstract-level only):** Farabursen increases urinary polycystin-1 and polycystin-2 (pharmacodynamic evidence of mechanism engagement) and reduces height-adjusted total kidney volume (htTKV) growth.
- **Significance:** First disclosed clinical signal for polycystin restoration as a therapeutic strategy in ADPKD. First non-tolvaptan drug to show a structural kidney endpoint signal in patients.
- **Limitations:** Abstract-only disclosure — no sample size, effect magnitude, statistical precision, or safety detail in the public record. Phase 1 is primarily a safety/PK study. Company-sponsored. Full peer-reviewed publication and Phase 2 program details are pending.

### MANGROVE — GLPG2737 CFTR Inhibitor Phase 2a (NCT04578548) — NEGATIVE (May 2026)
- **Result:** Negative. Full results published in *Nephrology, Dialysis and Transplantation*, May 2026 (Gansevoort et al., pm_42201338). Trial terminated for lack of efficacy.
- **Design:** Phase 2a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial, 2:1 allocation. GLPG2737 150 mg/day vs. placebo, n=66 ADPKD patients, 52 weeks. Sponsor: Galapagos NV.
- **Key findings:** htTKV growth rate: GLPG2737 +8.2% vs. placebo +9.2%, difference −0.91%, p=0.61 — not significant. eGFR change: difference −2.31 mL/min/1.73 m², p=0.17 — not significant. The drug was well tolerated; both the double-blind period and the open-label extension were terminated for lack of efficacy.
- **Significance:** CFTR (cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator) had strong preclinical rationale for ADPKD — it drives chloride secretion into cysts and is expressed in cyst-lining epithelial cells. Companion preclinical work showed GLPG2737 prevented cyst growth in multiple mouse models. The clinical failure adds CFTR channel inhibition to the list of preclinically-supported approaches that did not translate to human ADPKD (alongside statins, somatostatin analogues for kidney modification, venglustat, and mTOR inhibitors). See 01-pharmacological-treatments.md for the broader discussion.
- **What remains open:** Other CFTR modulators at different doses, combination approaches, or targeting CFTR's non-channel scaffold functions (e.g., HIPPO pathway interactions shown in preclinical work) are not excluded by this trial.
- **Note:** Trial completed April 2023; full publication appeared May 2026 (~3-year lag consistent with delayed reporting of negative results).

### LIPS — Lanreotide Phase 3 (NCT02127437) — NEGATIVE (published August 2026)
- **Result:** Negative on primary endpoint. Full results published in *Kidney International*, 2026 -- the trial itself completed data collection back in 2019, so this is a long-delayed publication, not a new trial.
- **Design:** 3-year, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of monthly lanreotide 120mg in 144 adults with stage 2/3 ADPKD (mGFR 30–89). Enrollment stopped early at 144 of a planned 180 participants due to slow recruitment.
- **Key findings:** No significant difference in **measured** (iohexol-clearance) GFR decline between lanreotide and placebo (annualized difference −0.3 mL/min/1.73m²/yr, 95% CI −3.4 to +2.8). A positive signal on the routine creatinine-based estimated-GFR test did **not** hold up when kidney function was checked with cystatin C or a urine-based measurement in the same patients. New safety signal: hypoglycemia in 11.1% of lanreotide-treated patients vs. 1.4% on placebo.
- **Significance:** The strongest single piece of evidence yet that somatostatin analogues (as a class) do not protect kidney function in ADPKD, corroborating an earlier meta-analysis of octreotide trials that reached the same conclusion. See 01-pharmacological-treatments.md for the full discussion, including why the creatinine-vs-cystatin-C discrepancy matters.
- **Limitation:** Early enrollment stoppage widened the confidence interval, so a small effect cannot be completely excluded either way.

### CAM2029 — Octreotide Depot, Polycystic Liver Disease Phase 2/3 (NCT05281328) — marginal result (posted July 2026)
- **Result:** Marginal positive on liver volume; not a kidney trial. Registry-posted; not yet peer-reviewed.
- **Design:** CAM2029 (Camurus subcutaneous octreotide depot) vs. placebo in polycystic **liver** disease, n=71.
- **Key findings:** Height-adjusted total liver volume fell 0.7% on treatment vs. rose 3.9% on placebo at Week 53 (estimated relative difference −4.3%, 95% CI −8.4 to −0.1, p=0.0443 -- just barely under the conventional significance threshold, and driven mainly by the placebo group's liver continuing to grow rather than the treatment group's liver shrinking). All 13 secondary endpoints, including kidney volume and eGFR, remain unreported. One death occurred as a serious adverse event in the treatment arm.
- **Significance:** This is an industry-sponsored trial (Camurus) of the company's own product, in **liver** disease, not kidney disease -- it should not be read as evidence for or against somatostatin analogues' effect on kidney function (see LIPS, above, for that question).
- **Limitation:** Fragile p-value, unreported secondary endpoints, a treatment-arm death, and no peer review yet. The study continues to Week 173.

### Renal Denervation RDN-ADPKD (TCT 2025)
- **Status:** Preliminary results first presented at TCT 2025 (October 2025) and indexed in JACC (2026). Open-label, small n, pre-post design, commercial sponsor, blood pressure endpoint.
- **Significance:** Hypothesis-generating only. Not a definitive test of renal denervation in ADPKD hypertension.

### Tolvaptan Post-Hoc Analyses and Registries
- Multiple analyses from the TEMPO 3:4, REPRISE, and long-term extension studies continue to be published, refining understanding of which patients benefit most from tolvaptan, long-term safety profiles, and optimal dosing strategies. These are not new trials but ongoing analyses of existing data.

## Key Trends in the ADPKD Pipeline (April 2026)

### 1. Unprecedented Pharma Industry Engagement
For the first time, multiple major pharmaceutical companies -- Vertex, AbbVie, AstraZeneca, and GSK -- are simultaneously running ADPKD-specific clinical trials. Historically, Otsuka (maker of tolvaptan) was the only large pharma company with an approved ADPKD drug. This influx of industry investment signals that ADPKD is now seen as a viable commercial market, which should accelerate drug development and expand treatment options.

### 2. Shift Toward Precision Medicine
The Vertex VX-407 (AGLOW) trial represents a paradigm shift. Instead of treating all ADPKD patients the same, this approach targets a specific subset of PKD1 mutations. This mirrors the cystic fibrosis revolution, where Vertex developed mutation-specific modulators that transformed outcomes. If successful, it could lead to genotype-guided treatment in ADPKD -- making genetic testing even more important for newly diagnosed patients.

### 3. Metabolic Reprogramming as a Therapeutic Strategy
Multiple independent lines of evidence now converge on the idea that ADPKD cyst cells have altered metabolism (increased glucose dependence, reduced fatty acid oxidation). This insight has spawned an entire class of trials:
- **Ketogenic diet** (dietary glucose restriction)
- **Exogenous ketones** (direct ketone body supplementation)
- **Metformin** (AMPK activation, improved cellular energy metabolism)
- **SGLT2 inhibitors** (altered renal glucose handling)
- **GLP-1 receptor agonists** (metabolic and anti-inflammatory effects)

These approaches are not mutually exclusive and could potentially be combined. They also tend to use existing, well-characterized drugs, which speeds development.

### 4. Strong European and Dutch Contribution
Several key trials are led by European, and specifically Dutch, institutions:
- **HYDROPROTECT** -- UMCG, Groningen (Netherlands)
- **Leuprorelin for PLD** -- UMCG, Groningen (Netherlands)
- **STOP-PKD** -- University of Cologne (Germany)
- **Renal Denervation** -- University of Erlangen-Nurnberg (Germany)

The UMCG in Groningen is a globally recognized center for both ADPKD and polycystic liver disease research. European regulatory pathways (EMA) may provide earlier access to treatments tested in EU-based trials.

### 5. Beyond the Kidney: Liver Disease Getting Attention
The leuprorelin trial for polycystic liver disease reflects growing recognition that ADPKD is a systemic disease. Liver cysts cause significant morbidity in many patients, particularly women, yet have received less research attention historically. Somatostatin analogues (octreotide, lanreotide) are used off-label in some countries, but a dedicated trial of a hormonal approach is a welcome addition.

## What This Means for Patients (April 2026)

The ADPKD treatment landscape is more active and more hopeful than at any previous point in history. Key takeaways:

1. **Tolvaptan remains the only approved disease-modifying therapy** (as of April 2026), but the pipeline behind it is deep and diverse.

2. **Ask your nephrologist about trial eligibility.** Many of these trials are actively recruiting. Participation helps advance science and may provide access to novel therapies. The PKD Foundation maintains a trial-matching service.

3. **Genetic testing is becoming more important.** The VX-407 trial specifically requires certain PKD1 mutations. As more targeted therapies emerge, knowing your specific mutation type will increasingly influence treatment options.

4. **Dietary approaches are being taken seriously by researchers.** The KETO-ADPKD results and follow-up trials suggest that metabolic interventions may play a role, but patients should discuss any major dietary changes with their healthcare team. Self-directed ketogenic diets carry risks (kidney stone formation, electrolyte disturbances) that need medical monitoring.

5. **Multiple independent strategies are in play.** Even if some trials fail, the diversity of approaches (metabolic, genetic, hormonal, procedural) means that the probability of at least one new approved therapy in the next 5-10 years is high.

6. **Watch for 2027 as a pivotal year.** Several Phase 2 trials and the large IMPEDE-PKD Phase 3 trial are expected to read out, which could significantly reshape the treatment landscape.
